# The herald (`app/herald`) Context

## Metadata
- Domain: late.sh's social accounts. One picture a day, posted by the server through an ordinary account on each network it is on (Mastodon, Bluesky), so the things members make to be seen (the Artboard, the gallery's pieces) and the rituals they share (the arcade's week, the daily paper) are seen outside the terminal.
- Status: Active, live. Both accounts post from prod: `@late@fosstodon.org` and `late.sh` on Bluesky. Prod pins both; the `herald_settings` row is on, and the operator flips it (below).

## What it is

An arrivals lever built to the shape in `AUDIENCE.md` ("The herald"): post, do not federate. One account per network, a token each, a few HTTP calls a day. The only session side is the admin's `/herald`; nothing a member does triggers a post. It reads what members already made public (the shared board, the pieces they hung, the public boards and the paper) and never chat. One post is picked, worded and fetched once, then delivered to every configured network, so a change to what it says or shows changes it everywhere; the one difference between networks is the mark, and on Mastodon a marked picture becomes a note (below).

## Module map

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `svc.rs` | `HeraldService`: the checker on every replica (`start_task`, `check`), `work_day` (the claim, the stored pick or a fresh one with its look, the picture fetch, one delivery per network, the settle), `find` (one read per kind: `find_piece`, `find_board`, `find_paper`, the scoreboard on Sundays), `look_at` (the look run, counted, a failure taken as `Unseen`), and the admin's commands (`request` runs `run_command` off the session and answers over a broadcast channel; `tick` drains the answer into a banner and hands `/herald` from the composer to the service); every log line, metric and error type. |
| `state.rs` | Pure rules: `post_due_day` and `HERALD_POST_TIME` (16:00 UTC), `shown_day` (the key itself is `Snapshot::daily_key`), `scoreboard_week` (Sundays), `piece_window_start` (`PIECE_WAIT_DAYS`) and `picked_pieces`, `BoardInk` (the board's drawn cells against `BOARD_MIN_INK`, and `drawn_area`, the crop), the candidates (`BoardFound`, `PieceCandidate`, `ScoreboardCandidate`, `PaperCandidate`) and `Found`, whose `picture` is the rotation; `Picture` (its kind, route, page and subject, serialized onto the row; a new kind is one more variant and every match learns it), `Warning` and `Look` (what the look saw, serialized onto the row), the per-network marks (`mastodon_post`, a picture or a note; `bluesky_mark` with its `Words`), `status_text`, `note_text` and `alt_text`; `HeraldCommand` and `parse_herald_command` for `/herald`; `HeraldState`, the session's handle and receiver. Reads no clock, does no I/O. |
| `look.rs` | The look: one Gemini vision call (`AiService::generate_json_with_images`, `AI_MODEL`) with the picture whole and in four `quarters` for the kinds members drew (the board, a piece), the prompt tuned by hand over the gallery's archived boards with a paragraph per kind, the schema answer `{ alt, warning, reason }` and its `parse` into `Look` (an unknown warning or an empty alt refused, the alt cut at `ALT_MAX_CHARS`). A card (the scoreboard, the paper) is `Drawn` and AI off is `Unseen`, both without a call. Returns the look or an error; logs nothing. |
| `mastodon.rs` | The Mastodon leg: `deliver` is, for a picture, the media upload (200, or 202 polled until ready) then the status call; for a note, the status call alone behind its content warning. The day's key is the `Idempotency-Key` either way. Returns the status URL; logs nothing. |
| `bluesky.rs` | The Bluesky leg: `deliver` is the fit (`fit_under` shrinks the PNG under `BLOB_MAX_BYTES` with the `image` crate, on the blocking pool, before any call), a session from the app password, the blob upload, then `putRecord` under `tid_for(key)` with `facets` for the link and the tags and the look's self-label. Returns the bsky.app URL; logs nothing. |

## Data

- `herald_posts` (model `late-core/src/models/herald.rs`, migrations 252 to 256): one row per UTC day, the claim. `id` is minted at the first claim and never changes: Mastodon sees it as the `Idempotency-Key`, Bluesky as the record key derived from it. `posting` is held while the calls run; `kind` (`artboard`, `piece`, `scoreboard`, `paper`; migration 256), `subject` (the board's `daily:YYYY-MM-DD` key, `piece:{id}`, `week:{last day}`, `paper:{edition}`; `subjects_since` reads a kind's back, which is how a piece goes out once), `picture` (the serialized `Picture`) and `look` (the serialized `Look`) are written at pick time in one statement (`store_pick`), before the first call, so a retry delivers the same picture with the same alt text and mark and never asks the model again; a check holds `picture` and `look` both or neither, and the rows picked before migration 255 read `"unseen"`; `posted` means every configured network delivered; `skipped` is a day with nothing worth posting; `failed` keeps `attempts`. Claim semantics as `job_press_runs` and the paper's rows, plus a window: a stale `posting` (older than `HERALD_STALE_CLAIM`, 20 minutes) is taken over and a `failed` row is claimed again, both under `HERALD_MAX_ATTEMPTS` (3) and only while the day's first claim (`first_claimed_at`) is younger than `HERALD_CLAIM_WINDOW` (50 minutes); past the window a stale `posting` rests as `failed`, and the day is finished either way.
- `herald_deliveries`: one row per network a day's picture landed on, written as each call succeeds; a retry sends only to the networks without one.
- `herald_settings` (`HeraldSwitch` in the same model): the operator's switch, one row keyed `posting`, read at every check, seeded off by migration 252. Herald-owned like `greendragon_settings`; the process-wide `app_flags` table was dropped in migration 219 and stays dropped.
- Reads of other domains, through model surfaces only: the board's key is `Snapshot::daily_key` (the rollover writes under the same call) and the board comes from `Snapshot::find_by_board_key` and is decoded with `dartboard_core::Canvas` to count and locate its ink; a piece from `ArtboardPiece::newest_hung_since` (still up, hung inside the window, not picked yet); the week from `leaderboard::week_bests`; the edition from `PaperEdition::load` (a `ready` OUTSIDE or WHAT WE WERE READING column, the ones the web prints).

## The post

- **When.** Every replica checks every `HERALD_CHECK_INTERVAL` (5 minutes). The day's post is due at `HERALD_POST_TIME`, 16:00 UTC (late afternoon in Europe, noon on the US east coast); `post_due_day` names the day owed, so a replica that was down at the hour catches up and stamps the right day. A settled day is memoized per replica, so the rest of the day costs one flag read per check and nothing else.
- **What.** One kind a day, by the rotation (`Found::picture`): on Sundays the arcade's week to Saturday (`week_bests`: per game the most daily wins and the top score, whoever played), else the newest gallery piece hung in the last `PIECE_WAIT_DAYS` (7) that no day picked yet, else yesterday's daily Artboard snapshot once it has `BOARD_MIN_INK` (200) drawn cells (the board is wiped on the first of the month, and the days after it are a few strokes), else the Late Edition dated the day when a public column printed, else nothing: a `skipped` day, settled, not retried. The scoreboard goes first because it has one day a week and a piece can wait a day; the window keeps a backlog from crowding out the board for weeks and the launch from posting a year of pieces. A piece is picked once, whatever came of its day. The poster kind of the first design was dropped before launch: too few new posters to carry a slot.
- **Where the picture comes from.** late-web's PNG routes (`late-web/CONTEXT.md`), fetched once per attempt over `HeraldConfig::web_internal_url` (the cluster service in prod, the compose service in dev): the board at `/gallery/{key}/image.png?crop=x,y,w,h`, cropped to its drawn cells plus `CROP_MARGIN` and rendered at the crop's own scale, so a sparse board is a few strokes, not a dark field; a piece at `/gallery/pieces/{id}/image.png`; the scoreboard at `/arcade/week/{last}/image.png` and the paper at `/paper/{edition}/image.png`, both text cards late-web draws from the same rows. One render a day on service-web's CPU.
- **The look.** At pick time, after the fetch and before the store, the picture goes to the model whole and in four quarters (the whole board reaches it shrunk, and only the quarters showed it the small doodles in the hand run) with a schema answer: a description (`alt`), one warning from the closed set (`none`, `nudity`, `suggestive`, `explicit`, `gore`, `offensive`) and the piece that set it (`reason`, for the operator: the `/herald preview` banner). The prompt in `look.rs` labels what is drawn, never small text or jokes in words (a slur drawn large is the one exception), never identity (a pride flag, the word gay), and counts a drawn penis, even a doodle, as explicit. A piece gets the same rules with its own paragraph (one member's titled work, not a collage). The look never skips a post; it decides the alt text and the mark, and with the mark how Mastodon shows it. A failed call or AI off is `Unseen`: the template alt text and the mark `not reviewed`, never unmarked. The cards are `Drawn`: late.sh's own words, clean by construction, the alt text written from the same rows, no call. One call on a board or piece day (plus previews), about 6,000 input tokens and up to 16,384 out, most of it thinking.
- **The words.** One text, under 300 graphemes (Bluesky's limit, the tightest; Mastodon allows 500), marks included: a line of plain speech, the page (the gallery for the board, not the day's snapshot, which the Artboard keeps for seven days; the piece's page; the edition; the front page for the scoreboard, whose boards live in the terminal), the kind's tags (the board and pieces `#ASCIIart #MastoArt #tildeverse #terminal`, the scoreboard `#puzzles #sudoku #tildeverse #terminal`, the paper `#tildeverse #terminal #smolweb`). A piece credits its member as `~name`, never `@name`, since a bare `@` is a mention of whoever has that name on the instance; nothing else names anyone. The same alt text on every network: `ASCII art: ` and the look's description for the board and pieces, the card's own words for the cards.
- **The mark.** Fosstodon bans adult and violent content even behind a content warning, so Mastodon gets the picture only when the look is clean or drawn; any warning, or not reviewed, posts a note instead (`MastodonPost::Note`, `note_text`): no media, `sensitive`, the warning as its content warning (`nsfw: nudity`, `nsfw: suggestive`, `nsfw: explicit`, `cw: gore`, `cw: offensive`, `cw: not reviewed`), words that say what the post was and that the picture is on late.sh and Bluesky, the front page as its link and only `#tildeverse #terminal`. The link is never the picture's own page: a status without media gets a preview card from its link, and the gallery's card is the board's PNG, which would put the picture on the server anyway; the front page's card is the site's own picture. Bluesky has self-labels, not warnings: `nudity`, `sexual`, `porn`, `graphic-media` on the record's `labels`, and every marked post swaps its last tag for `#nsfw` (people there mute the tag). Offensive and not reviewed have no label there, so a first line `cw: offensive` or `cw: not reviewed` and the tag are the whole mark, and the image is not blurred. Both maps are exhaustive matches on `Look` in `state.rs`.
- **Mastodon.** A picture: `POST /api/v2/media` as multipart (`file`, `description`), 200 with the attachment or 202 polled on `GET /api/v1/media/{id}` until 200; then `POST /api/v1/statuses` with `status`, `media_ids`, `visibility` (`MastodonConfig::visibility`: public in prod, unlisted in dev), `language: en` and the `Idempotency-Key` header. A note: the status call alone, `media_ids` empty, `sensitive` and `spoiler_text`. The status's `url` is the delivery's.
- **Bluesky.** `com.atproto.server.createSession` with the handle and the app password, `com.atproto.repo.uploadBlob` with the PNG (shrunk by 20% a side per pass until under `BLOB_MAX_BYTES`, 950,000, the PDS's cap being a megabyte), then `com.atproto.repo.putRecord` of an `app.bsky.feed.post` with the text, `langs`, the image embed (alt, blob, aspect ratio) and facets (`#link` for the page, `#tag` per hashtag, by UTF-8 byte range). The record key is a TID built from the day's UUID v7 (its millisecond timestamp and ten bits of its randomness), so the same day puts the same record. The delivery's URL is `https://bsky.app/profile/{handle}/post/{rkey}`. Bluesky has no unlisted post: a dev post there is public.
- **Never.** Chat, a frame of #lounge, anything a member did not make to be seen. Two posts a day on one network, or a post skipped for what it shows: a marked picture is a note on Mastodon, never nothing, and removal is a hand job from the account.

## Config and switches

- `Config::herald` (`HeraldConfig` in `late-ssh/src/config.rs`): `mastodon: Option<MastodonConfig>` (instance, token, visibility), `bluesky: Option<BlueskyConfig>` (pds, identifier, app password), `web_internal_url`. A network configured is the herald on (`enabled()`); none is off. Prod pins `https://fosstodon.org` and the Bluesky handle `late.sh` as literals and requires `LATE_HERALD_MASTODON_TOKEN` and `LATE_HERALD_BLUESKY_PASSWORD`, injected from the `herald-credentials` secret (`infra/secrets.tf`, `infra/service-ssh.tf`; GitHub secrets `HERALD_MASTODON_TOKEN` and `HERALD_BLUESKY_PASSWORD` through both terraform workflows). A new network is the same shape: pin its literal, `required` its secret, add the variable, the secret key, the env and the two workflow lines, shipped as an `-infra` release before the ssh image that needs it (`infra/RUNBOOK.md`, deploy skew rule). Dev: each network on only with its pair in `.env.local` (`LATE_HERALD_MASTODON_INSTANCE` + `..._TOKEN`, `LATE_HERALD_BLUESKY_IDENTIFIER` + `..._PASSWORD`; half a pair refuses to start), `web_internal_url` the compose web service, Mastodon unlisted. `Config::validate` refuses a configured network with an empty credential or a non-HTTP URL.
- The row: `/herald on` and `/herald off` in chat, admins only (the same statement through `scripts/connect_db.sh` works too); `off` stops the next check, no deploy. `on` first settles the day the switch slept through: before 16:00 the due day is yesterday, and with no row it is written `skipped` (zero attempts) before the row flips, so the first post after enabling is the next 16:00 and never a catch-up of a two-day-old board; after 16:00 the due day is today and, unposted, it goes out at the next check. The banner says which. The config is the deployment's switch; the row is the operator's.
- `/herald status` reads the row and today's day (waiting, posting, posted where, skipped, failed after how many attempts). `/herald preview` shows what today's post would be (the rotation's pick, by subject) and to which networks, and runs a fresh look at it (the warning, its reason, whether Mastodon gets the picture or a note, and the alt text in the banner, which a long alt overflows), sending nothing; with nothing to post it says what each kind lacked. `/herald post` works today's day now: it claims the row like the checker does, so a day goes out once however many admins type it, and the 16:00 check finds it settled. It ignores the row on purpose (an admin asking is the switch for that post) but not the config: with no network configured it says so. Every subcommand is refused with a banner for non-admins; the answer is one banner line (`svc::banner_for`), sent back over the service's channel to the session that asked.
- Account setup is by hand: Mastodon's bot flag and profile links in its web settings, the application with `write:media` and `write:statuses` under Development; Bluesky's app password under Settings, Privacy and security, and its automation label under Settings, "Show automation label" (the `bot` self-label on the profile record). The landing page on late-web links both accounts with `rel="me"`, which is what the Mastodon profile's verified check reads.

## Telemetry

- `late_ssh_herald_posts_total{result}`: `posted`, `skipped`, `lost` (a check that found the day held by another replica; a held day is not memoized, so it counts per check until the holder settles it), `failed` (the post, or the claim itself, failed). `late_ssh_herald_deliveries_total{network,result}`: `delivered`, `failed` per leg. `late_ssh_herald_looks_total{result}`: `clean`, `warned`, `unseen`, `drawn` (a card, no call), per look run (a day's fresh pick or a preview; a retry reuses the stored look and counts nothing). Grafana "Herald" row, `monitoring/dashboards/observability.json`.
- Spans `herald.check` around every check and `herald.command` around an admin's command. Error types `herald_flag_failed`, `herald_claim_failed`, `herald_delivery_failed` (per network, once, with the network), `herald_post_failed` (the day's summary), `herald_command_failed` (a command's read or flip failed), `herald_look_failed` (the model's error, once; the post goes out `Unseen`); info lines `the herald delivered` (day, network, url), `the herald posted` (day, kind, subject, warning, urls) and `the herald had nothing worth posting`; a warn when a failed day's row cannot be marked.

## Gotchas

- **Two idempotency mechanisms, one key, one window.** Mastodon keeps an `Idempotency-Key` for about an hour, so every retry of a day has to fall inside it: a day is never claimed again once its first claim is older than `HERALD_CLAIM_WINDOW` (50 minutes), whatever `attempts` says, and a stale `posting` row past the window rests as `failed` for the operator. Inside the window a lost answer is retried under the same key (stale after 20 minutes, three claims at most) and Mastodon answers with the status it already made; Bluesky's `putRecord` under the same TID is an upsert at any time. A delivery that landed but could not be recorded is sent again under the same key, which both networks dedupe. So a lost answer never double-posts; a day that dies past the window is a hand job, never a retry.
- **The picture is fetched per attempt, not per network**, and the pick is stored before the first call: a retry after a partial failure shows the same picture on the network that missed it.
- **The look marks, it never removes.** A slur drawn at 23:50 still ships: a note behind `cw: offensive` on Mastodon, the picture under `#nsfw` on Bluesky; the herald's copy on a network is removed by hand from the account, and so is a piece's post when the piece comes down (its web page 404s at once). The model misses things: in the hand run over the archived boards it named a poster wrongly, and only the quarters made it see a small drawn penis. Its labels vary between runs on the same board (nudity one run, suggestive the next), so a day's mark is one reading, not a verdict.
- **Most boards this month carry a warning.** The live board keeps its drawings until the monthly wipe, so one nude figure marks every daily until the first of the month, and every one of those days is a note on Mastodon. Fosstodon's rules (`/api/v1/instance/rules` and its code of conduct at `hub.fosstodon.org/coc`): "DO NOT post adult or age-restricted content. This includes nudity and other sexualized or violent content", with no exception for a content warning, plus no slurs, alt text on images, and automated accounts monitored by a member. Whether a note behind a warning linking off-server is within them is its moderators' call; the note carries no picture and no link that unfurls into one. The other kinds give Mastodon pictures: pieces are mostly clean, the cards always are.
- **`/herald post` posts today, the checker posts the due day.** Before 16:00 the checker owes yesterday and the admin's command works today (with yesterday's board), so a hand post in the morning and the checker's catch-up of yesterday can both happen on one day; after 16:00 they are the same day and the row keeps it to one post.
- **The human half is operator work.** Answer replies, boost members, follow people (`AUDIENCE.md`). Fosstodon's rule for automated accounts is that an active member runs them; Bluesky's is that a bot never interacts unprompted, so boosting members there is done from a person's own account.
