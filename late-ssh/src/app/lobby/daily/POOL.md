# Daily pool

Eight-ball, nine-ball and snooker as daily correspondence matches (`pool.rs`, `pool_ui.rs`, `pool_input.rs`, `pool_draft.rs`, `live_board.rs`; physics in `games/pool_core`). Part of the daily games domain (`CONTEXT.md`).

Pool is the roster's only simulated game family, and the one place the "a variant is one game with different setup" rule bends. They are **three `DailyGame` variants sharing one state type (`pool.rs`), one detail struct and one renderer, but with separate rulesets**: their rules genuinely differ, so `PoolRules` is a field on the state rather than a second struct. Everything below the ruleset (table, rack format, physics, shot, renderers) is shared and lives in `app/games/pool_core`, so a live pool hall as a house table would reuse it verbatim.
  - **The physics is the referee.** A shot is `(optional placement, azimuth, tip offset, speed, called pocket)` and nothing else; the server re-simulates it from the stored rack. A doctored client can send an illegal *shot* and never an illegal *result*. This is also why pool is the one roster game with **no optimistic apply**: every other game can work out its own result locally, but a pool result is a simulation, and running it on two machines to see whether they agree is exactly the coupling this split avoids. The board waits for the reload; `PoolDetail::shot_in_flight` blocks a second shot in the meantime.
  - **What is persisted:** `rack` (where the balls are now, authoritative), `prev_rack` (where they were before the last shot), and the `Shot` inputs. `prev_rack` is exactly enough to re-simulate and animate the last shot, which is the only shot anyone watches; a full timeline would be megabytes of JSONB per move. The table preset is stored **by name** and resolved through `table::preset`, so retuning a coefficient surfaces as an error on the match that needs it rather than silently reinterpreting a rack that was never played on that equipment.
  - **Every game breaks from in hand.** Snooker breaks from the D, because it is the rule. The pool games break from the *kitchen*: `opening_ball_in_hand` is the one place that says so, `DailyPoolState::new` and `rewound` both read it, and the break spot is only where the ball starts. A bar player picks their spot on the break, and without it the break would be the one shot in the rack where the board left the player nothing to decide but the speed. The permission is spent by the break like any other: `Ruling::pass()` leaves `ball_in_hand` as `None`, so the incoming player has one only if a foul said so.
  - **Potting keeps the table** (battleship's "a hit fires again"). That is what stops a rack costing as many days as it has balls: a present player runs out in one sitting and only a miss hands the clock over. **A match can be several frames**: the challenger picks best of 1/3/5/7 in the picker (`h`/`l`, cue games only), stored on the row as `daily_matches.best_of` (migration 239) because an open challenge has no state yet, and copied into `DailyPoolState::best_of` at claim. The frame rolls over *inside* `apply_shot`: when a frame ends short of a majority, the shot is recorded and the next rack is built at once, breaker alternating (`frame % 2`), from `frame_seed(seed, frame)`: derived, not drawn, so `replay` from the opening rack still reproduces every later rack and the service needed no new step (unlike gin's `deal_next`). `frame_first_shot` is what makes the second rack a break (`shots_taken` counts from it). `prev_rack` is left on the old table, so the frame-ending shot still animates before the new rack appears. Payout: `win_chips` = prize × `counted_frames[winner]` (min 1), per frame *won*, and only frames that ran to `FRAME_MIN_SHOTS` (5), because an eight-ball frame can be thrown in one stroke and would otherwise multiply a colluding pair's pair-day prize by the match length. `commit_pool_shot` hands `finish_events` the row with the state the shot wrote, or the count is a frame short.
  - **Ball in hand rides inside `Shot.place`**, so a foul costs the fouled player no extra turn of the correspondence clock. A potted cue ball makes placement *mandatory*: `sim::simulate` un-pots the cue ball unconditionally, so without that check a scratch would quietly resurrect it at the pocket mouth. `ShotMode::Place` (`m`) is how a player sets it down, and a scratched board **opens already holding it** rather than looking broken. **Any foul opens the board already holding the ball**: every foul in both rulesets grants one, not just a scratch (no contact, wrong ball first and no-rail all do), and a player who is not told they have it plays the leave they were left. A left click sets it down *and* ends the mode (`place_and_commit`), because left click means "there, done" everywhere else on this board. A cue ball that was actually potted also starts at a legal spot near the break spot rather than nowhere, so the board is never showing a table with no cue ball on it. A click **snaps to the nearest legal spot** through `rules::free_spot` instead of being refused: the table view is an overview where a ball is a few pixels, so an exact click is not something a player can be asked for. The held ball is drawn at its pending spot (`PoolDraft::frames`, which is also what the shot line is cast against), because the one thing a player needs while placing it is to see where it will land: and it **follows the pointer live** while `Place` is armed (`pool_hover_table`, fed from `board_input` because only the caller can turn a cell into a spot on the cloth). It is the one mode the pointer steers *through the table geometry* rather than as a bare delta; `PoolDraft::pointer_moved` therefore reports `Ignored` for `Place`. **The line follows the ball it is on while the cue ball is carried** (`PoolDraft::carry`): ball in hand is played as pick the ball, then walk the cue ball until the shot is straight, and a fixed bearing swung the line onto a rail at every step. A line on a bare spot of cloth is a bearing and stays one.
  - **Nothing about a shot is sequenced.** Target, spin, aim and stroke are all live at once and the player may strike whenever they like; a key only says what the *pointer* is steering right now (`ShotMode`). `[`/`]` cycle the legal targets and `'` jumps to the lowest one that is on; `h`/`l` turn the cue a degree and `H`/`L` a tenth of one; a click picks any spot on the cloth; `a` arms aim, `e` arms spin, `m` takes ball in hand, and `x`/`s`/`w` arm the stroke light, normal or strong. Three gestures, three meanings:
    - **Left click commits**: keep the adjustment, put the cue down. Space is its keyboard twin. It is taken on the *release* and only when the press did not travel, because a press that moves is a **re-grip**: the mouse lifted off the pad, the reference following the pointer while the setting stays put. That is the only way to keep turning an aim once the pointer has run out of screen (a terminal reports motion only inside its own window), and until the button comes up there is no telling the two gestures apart.
    - **Right click resets**: zero what is armed (centre ball, dead-on aim, the held ball back where it lay: its opening spot if it was potted, otherwise no placement at all) and *stay in the mode*. With **nothing** armed it clears the whole shot: aim and spin both back to square. That case is the common one: you look at the panel, decide against the english, and there is nothing to re-arm and undo because you already put the cue down. `c` is the keyboard twin. "Put the spin back to centre" is what a player reaches for far more often than "undo the last few pixels", and centre-ball is fiddly to walk back to on a face a few pixels across. A stroke has no meaningful neutral, so there it just puts the cue down.
    - **Esc cancels**: restore the value the mode was armed with and drop the mode. All three adjustable values come back from one snapshot taken at arming time, so a snapshot can never fall out of step with the mode it belongs to; arming a second mode commits the first and re-snapshots, so a cancelled spin cannot undo a settled aim. With nothing armed, Esc leaves the board like every other game.

    **The pointer has a fine gear.** A terminal cell is a coarse unit to aim in (one column of travel is about a ball's width at a metre and a half), so holding Ctrl while the pointer moves picks `AimGear::Fine`, a tenth of the travel. It scales the *travel* rather than the rate, so it applies to whichever delta-steered mode is armed (aim and spin both). There is no fast gear: xterm and most descendants keep Shift+mouse for their own selection and never send the report, so a Shift gear did nothing on most terminals. `MouseModifiers` comes off the SGR button byte, which `input.rs` already parses.

    **A mouse stroke's power is the push speed.** The mouse is the cue: `stroke_to` needs `MIN_BACKSWING` (two rows) of draw only to have something to push from, then times the forward push (`Push`: samples of row and arrival time) over the last `PUSH_WINDOW` (120 ms) before the cue passes the ball, and strikes at `PowerBand::speed_at` of that speed read on a log scale between `SLOW_PUSH_SPEED` (12 rows/s, the band's floor) and `FAST_PUSH_SPEED` (200, its ceiling); each strike logs its measured rows/s at debug level (`pool stroke pushed through`) for tuning. Log rather than linear, and a floor under every band, because a linear 0-100 scale made both a crawl and a slam too easy to hit by accident. Arrival times are SSH arrival times, so a flick that lands as one report is back-dated to the previous report (at most `PUSH_SEED`, 40 ms) and never timed under `PUSH_MIN_DT` (20 ms): fast, not infinite. `PoolDraft::power` is a speedometer during the push (the band's floor while drawing back); the keyboard keeps pull-is-power (`nudge_pull` clears `Push::by_mouse`). The two push speeds are the dial: rows per second depend on the player's font size and mouse DPI, which nothing here can see. The cue panel still draws the pull one terminal row per pointer row (`cue_ui::pull_travel`).

    **The aim is a bearing and nothing else** (`PoolDraft::azimuth`). Every aiming control turns it: the pointer's left/right in aim mode, `h`/`l`, the arrows, a click on a ball or the cloth (which points dead at the spot), and `[`/`]`/`'` (dead at the ball's centre). Which ball the line is on, the ghost, the object ball's short leg, the stun line and a miss's rebound are all read back off the table through `pool_core::aim` on every draw, never stored, so the picture and the shot the server receives cannot disagree, and the shot line runs to the first thing it meets rather than stopping at a chosen point. The one piece of remembered targeting is `picked`, the ball last chosen by name: the brackets step from it rather than from the sighted ball, because a ball picked behind another has the line stop at the one in front, and stepping from the sighted ball would step onto the same ball for ever. The share (`PoolAimShare`) carries the bearing, so a watcher rebuilds the identical line. The eye view stands on the bearing too: turning the cue turns the room and the cue ball's leg always runs straight up the middle of the screen. Up/down pointer travel does nothing in aim mode: there is one axis to an aim, and the re-grip is how a pointer that has run out of screen keeps turning.

    There is no hint row. What the *mouse* does now is said on the status line beside the mode's name, from `ShotMode::hint`, which lives beside the enum in `pool_core` so the controls and the description of them cannot drift; `pool_ui::shooter_spans` builds that part of the line and drops whichever half would repeat the other (the placing prompts while the ball is carried, the aiming hint while the ball is off the table), and a test lays out every mode against every `Prompt` and holds the line under `MIN_WIDTH`. The keyboard, all of it, is a legend in the right column under the readouts (`pool_ui::LEGEND` plus `exit_row`, `column_split`), and only there. It teaches only the keys that act (`LegendKeys`, the same gate as `pool_draft_mut`): every control at the table, the camera and resign while waiting or while a shot plays, the camera and the way out for a spectator or a finished match. Also there: the cue drawing is capped at `MAX_CUE_ROWS` and the legend takes the rows below, all or nothing, once the cue drawing keeps `MIN_CUE_ROWS_WITH_LEGEND`, which is low enough that the smallest board shows it. The right column has no border of its own; the table's border is the seam. The readouts under the cue drawing are the bearing, what the line is on (`target_label`: the ball and the cut, never where the object ball ends up, a snooker ball named with its value, `the pink (6)`, for tables too small to print it; the rail; a pocket), spin and stroke.
  - **The panel's zones do not touch.** `click_cue_panel` reads `PanelHit::tip_radius` and `cue_top` rather than the ball's drawn radius: anywhere on the drawn ball places the tip (clamped, since the settable face is smaller than the ball), there is a strip of clear air below it that means "done", and only past that is the cue. If the face and the cue met at the ball's edge, a click aimed at maximum screw and landing one row low would arm the stroke, and on a half-block panel one terminal row is two pixels of ball.
  - **The cue panel is laid out as the shot, so its zones are the modes.** It draws the target ball at the top, the cue ball in front of you, and the cue below, so a click on the target ball or the sighting line arms *aim*, a click on the cue ball's face places the tip and commits *spin* in one gesture, and a click on the cue arms the *stroke* at the middle band. `cue_ui::draw` returns a `PanelHit` with all of that geometry and `board_input::click_cue_panel` reads it, so the hit test never reconstructs the panel's own layout. A press while a band is already armed is part of the pull and never a mode change. This is what lets a player who has read nothing start a shot: the picture tells them where to click.
  - **Why modes and not held keys.** A terminal sends key *presses* only: there is no key-up event without the Kitty keyboard protocol, which this build does not enable (`input.rs`), so "hold `a` and move the mouse" is not observable. Autorepeat is not a substitute: the first repeat lags half a second, the rate is a per-user OS setting, and SSH jitter smears it. What *is* available is bare pointer motion: `?1003h` any-event tracking is already on (`state.rs`), so the mouse steers the armed mode with no button held. Buttons are still needed for what a motion stream cannot express: picking a spot, committing, cancelling, and letting go of the cue. Right click is already load-bearing elsewhere in the app (Minesweeper flags, Solitaire auto-move, the Artboard's pan), so it is known to survive real terminals.
  - **The stroke is the real gesture: draw the pointer down, then push it back up through the ball.** Crossing the point where the stroke began is the moment of contact: no button anywhere in it. The power is the speed of the push (above), and a minimum backswing is required so that nudging the mouse upward on an armed cue cannot fire it. The stroke is also the one mode where pointer motion is read as an **absolute offset from where the gesture began** rather than as a delta: there the origin means something physical, it is where the cue ball is. Space fires from the keyboard, and arms the normal band first if nothing is armed, so the least-informed possible keypress still plays a sensible shot. `PowerBand` maps the same gesture into a different speed range: the whole gesture is spent inside the range the player asked for, instead of putting a delicate safety and a hard break a few pixels apart. **The bands are pinned to how hard people actually hit** rather than spread evenly over the range: the top of `normal` is 4.8 m/s, a firm shot that still breaks a snooker pack, not the two thirds of a break that made every shot feel like a slam (ranges in `app/games/pool_core/CONTEXT.md`). And the cue **stays thrown through the ball** from the moment the stroke registers until the shot has finished playing (`CueView::follow_through`, driven off `shot_in_flight || playback`, so there is no timer to fall out of step): a cue that snapped back to rest looked exactly like a stroke that never took.
  - Keyboard alone is enough for all of it (arrows drive whatever is armed, `h`/`l` and `H`/`L` turn the cue), which matters because the client's keyboard-only interaction mode ignores the mouse entirely. The mouse is the better path, not the required one; a cushion is reachable by turning onto it, and by a click.
  - **The cue ball's face is drawn magnified**: the whole drawn ball is the half-radius the tip may actually use, so the miscue circle is the ball's own edge and a click at the edge is the limit. A true-to-life face would spend most of a ball a few pixels wide drawing the region nobody is allowed to strike. The pointer rates (`TIP_PER_COLUMN`/`TIP_PER_ROW`) were halved to match, so the magnification buys precision rather than speed.
  - **An event repaints only the session it is visible to** (`EventEffect`, `state::apply_event`). The daily feed is process-wide, so every session on the replica receives every event; each arm reports whether it moved something *this* session draws, and `tick` sets `changed` from that rather than from an event having arrived. The aim update arrives eight times a second, so repainting on every arrival would rebuild a frame on every connected session (everyone in a chat room or a door game included) while one player lines up a shot, defeating the dirty gate in `ssh.rs` (`record_render_skipped_clean`) precisely when the replica is busiest. In the `AimChanged` arm the write and the repaint are deliberately one value: an aim that repaints without being drawn is the storm, one drawn without a repaint is an opponent's cue frozen mid-shot. Events about other people's matches are the lobby snapshot's business, and the snapshot raises its own flag at the top of the same tick.
  - **The opponent's aim is broadcast while they compose it.** `DailyEvent::AimChanged` carries a `PoolAimShare` (the bearing, tip, pull, mode, placement and called pocket; no target and no aim point travel, since a watcher rebuilds the whole line from the bearing) over the *existing* in-process broadcast channel; `DailyService` is process-global, so the aim needs no plumbing or table of its own. **The one event on this domain that is not a move**: everything else reports something durable, this reports what somebody is thinking about. Not persisted, not acknowledged, and a dropped event costs nothing because the next one carries the whole state. Publishing goes through one funnel (`board_input::handle_event` → `pool_publish_aim`) so a new control cannot be one forgotten line away from being invisible; `should_share_aim` throttles it to 120ms *except* on a mode change, since "they have armed the stroke" is the update whose timing is the information. The renderer always draws from a share (`PoolDraft::watching`), local or remote, so the two paths cannot drift.
  - **`v` swaps the overview for the shooter's eye** (`pool_core::table_3d`). It is a *camera*, not a control: allowed while watching, waiting or spectating, and while a shot plays out the eye stays where the shooter stood rather than following the ball. A camera that chases the cue ball round the table is not what watching a shot looks like. The view preference lives on `DailyBoardState`, not the detail, so it survives the reloads that rebuild the detail. The eye is render-recorded (`pool_eye_geometry`) exactly like the overview's rect, and `table_point_at` inverts whichever of the two was actually drawn: two views on one board is two chances for a click to land somewhere the picture did not put it. A click above the horizon is the room, and commits like any other click off the cloth.
  - **Animation** is the daily board's only one. **A reload carries a running playback across** (`PoolDetail::adopt`): a reload rebuilds the detail from the row and a fresh one has no playback, so a reload landing mid-animation deletes the animation. The shot that *ends* a rack publishes two events (`MovePlayed` and `MatchFinished`) and each asks for a reload, so the first started the shot playing and the second wiped it milliseconds later; every other shot publishes one event and survived by luck. That is what made a rack look like it ended with no final shot. It runs for `Timeline::visible_duration`, not the physical duration: friction is asymptotic, so a shot's last stretch is balls creeping fractions of a millimetre, and played back in full that is seconds of a table that looks settled while the board still refuses a shot. The status line also says `▶ the shot is playing` outright while it runs, because a player reading "Your shot" while nothing responds concludes the board is broken. Playback starts on **reload** for both players through one code path (`start_pool_playback`), gated on a shot count the board has not shown yet; the first load seeds that count so opening a match shows the table as it stands rather than replaying the shot that happened before you arrived. **The re-simulation does not run on the tick**: `start_pool_playback` gathers the inputs (`last_shot_sim`, local memory) and hands the physics to `spawn_blocking`, and `poll_pool_timeline` collects it a tick or two later. A shot is thousands of integration steps, and the tick path reads local memory only (root `CONTEXT.md` §3, Sync render, async services). A newer shot landing first simply replaces the receiver. While a shot rolls, `wake_hint` returns `HOT_TICK`: the single change pool makes outside the daily and games domains.
  - **The board says nothing the shot has not shown yet.** The reload carrying a shot brings the *settled* rack, and the animation of how it got there is a tick or two behind it on the blocking thread. Drawn as-is, that window would show the settled rack, the last-shot label, the status line's result and the win banner: the balls appear at their final spots, jump back, and then play out, so the result flashes up before the shot. `DailyBoardState::pool_shot_pending` is the flag that window hangs off: the table draws `PoolDetail::frames_before_shot` (the pre-shot rack), `status_line` keeps saying the shot is playing, and `pool_draft_mut`/`pool_send` refuse input, because a player aiming at that rack would be aiming at balls that have already moved. The panel's last-shot lines (`pool_ui::last_shot_lines`: the foul, the label, the free ball) stay off for longer than the window, through the whole animation: `PoolDetail::withholds_result` is true from the stroke until a fresh shot has finished playing, and false during a replay, which is watched with the result already known. The finish banner is the one piece that does not live on the board, so it is held separately: `PoolFinishHold`, taken while `pool_defers_finish` says the shot is still to play, with a 20s backstop (`FINISH_HOLD_MAX`) so news that is never released cannot be lost.
  - **Replay: `r` the last shot, `R` the whole last visit** (`ReplaySpan`). A correspondence game's problem is that you come back to a table and a score with nothing in between; the run of shots by one seat is exactly what you were not there for, so `visit_start` walks back to where that run started. **The two take different roads.** The last shot is one simulation off the stored `prev_rack` (`last_shot_sim`, the same inputs a fresh shot animates from), so it owes nothing to the history before it. A visit cannot be assembled from `prev_rack`: that is one rack and a visit is several shots, so `DailyPoolState::replay` rebuilds the opening rack from the stored `seed` (`rewound`) and plays the history forward through `apply_shot`, which is also the only way the re-spots a foul ordered land where they landed. **That road judges the whole match again by today's rules**, and each stored shot was judged by the rules of the day it was played, so `replay` returns a `ReplayError` rather than timelines of a match that never happened: `Refused` when `apply_shot` turns a stored shot down, `Diverged` when the history plays through and ends on a different rack than the stored one, `UnknownTable` for a preset this build no longer has. The board plays nothing in each case and the worker logs which (`replay_off_tick`), so changing a ruling takes `R` away from every stored match the change touches, and never `r`. Both ride the same machinery a fresh shot does (`spawn_blocking`, one receiver, and `poll_pool_timeline`), which is why that receiver carries a `Vec<Timeline>` and `PoolDetail` has a `queue` the finished playback pops from, so a visit runs as one break rather than four clips. Open to both players, a spectator and a finished match: it shows what has already been played and cannot become a move. Pressing the key again stops it, which is also how a player gets their board back. Stopping drops the receiver and not the worker, so `DailyBoardState::replay_worker` keeps the task's handle and a press that would start a replay while it is still running does nothing: a held key cannot queue a frame of physics per press pair on the blocking pool that simulates real shots.
  - **Pool resigns on `X`, not `r`.** Every other board resigns on `r`; on this one the key a player reaches for repeatedly is "show me that again", so `pool_key` takes `r`/`R` and puts resigning on `X`. **`pool_key` swallows `r`/`R` whether or not there is anything to replay**: the shared map behind it still resigns on `r`, so a replay key that fell through on an unbroken rack would ask to resign. `X` has that one job: the stroke keys `x`/`s`/`w` are lowercase only, so a held Shift or a Caps Lock cannot turn arming the light stroke into the first press of a resignation. The legend and the confirm prompt both name `X`. **The prompt is answered by `y` only**: `X` is Shift away from `x`, so while it is up any other key (a second `X` included) closes it and does nothing else, and Esc closes it without leaving the board (`cancel_pending_move` answers an open resign prompt first, on every board).
  - **The table's border says whose board it is and whether it is live**, in two channels: hue is the turn (green yours, red theirs, dim for a spectator or a finished rack) and brightness is whether anything can be done (bright at the table, dimmed while the balls roll). It is **always drawn**, so the table never changes size as the state changes: a border that appears and vanishes makes the whole board jump. It pairs with the `▶ the shot is playing` status line: between them there is no moment where the board's state is ambiguous. **That line outranks the result banner**, because the shot that ends a rack is exactly the one where announcing early is wrong: a pot is not a win until the rest of the shot has played out, since the cue ball can still follow the money ball down and hand the rack to the other player.
  - **The Lounge has a pool table** (`clubhouse::map::POOL_TABLE`, under the poker table). Enter on it opens the Lobby on the challenge picker with eight-ball selected, through `begin_challenge_draft_for`: walking up to a pool table is asking for pool, not for a list. It still opens the picker rather than posting outright, so the variant and the prize are in front of the player before anything is committed.
  - **The cloth carries a table's markings:** the baulk line, the D behind it, and the head and foot spots. Cosmetic except the baulk line, which is drawn from `rules::head_string`: the same number the kitchen rule is enforced against, so a player put in hand after a break scratch can see where "behind the line" ends.
  - **The equipment gives back what the terminal takes.** The bar box's pocket mouths are a tenth over life size (4.95 / 5.5 inches): aiming through a terminal costs a player accuracy the equipment never took from them, since a ball is a few pixels and the pointer moves in whole cells. Pockets are also *drawn* from the mouth width rather than from the exaggerated ball, so retuning a mouth changes how a pocket looks and not only how it plays: and every one of them straddles the table's edge, corners included (see `app/games/pool_core/CONTEXT.md`).
  - **Naming a pocket** is a click on the *pocket*, or `p` to step through them, and it only answers while `requires_call()`. "On the pocket" is two tests (`PoolDraft::call_pocket_at`): within a mouth's width of it, and at or past its mouth chord less three half-radii (`Geometry::pocket_depth`, the same line that decides a ball has dropped, with enough slack to cover the disc the pocket is *drawn* as). The depth test is the one that matters: a plain reach of one and a half mouths is a tenth of the table's length around each of six pockets, and with naming a pocket outranking picking a target, any click to pick the eight or to aim at a cushion near a rail would silently name a pocket instead whenever a shot had to be called. A click on the cloth is read in the order a player reads the table, **that ball, that pocket, that spot** (`PoolDraft::click_table`), and cloth in front of a pocket is somewhere to aim where the hole is not. `apply_shot` *refuses* an uncalled shot on the eight, so the draft is the only thing that can write `called_pocket`: without it every shot on the eight is rejected and no gesture fixes it. Tests that build a `Shot` by hand rather than going through the draft cannot see a dead board like this (the ball-in-hand one has the same shape), so drive shots through the draft.
  - **Snooker is the third ruleset on the same state type**, and the one the shape exists for: `Ruling` carries `points` and `balls_to_spot` for it. A full frame on a 12ft table, so `TableSpec` carries `head_string` and `d_radius` (a snooker baulk line is not a quarter of the table) and `BallInHand::TheD` puts the ball in hand from a semicircle, not from the whole baulk end. The snooker state fields `scores`, `on_colour`, `free_ball` and `may_return` are all `#[serde(default)]` so pool rows still parse. **A frame can end level**, which no other game here can do, so `finished` is separate from `winner` and the service maps the pair onto a draw. Pockets are cut to the bar box's generosity rather than a real snooker table's: a real one is *tighter* relative to the ball, and tight pockets on a table drawn a hundred columns wide would make the game about the rendering. Ball exaggeration needs no per-table calibration: it is a fixed pixel boost over true size, so a snooker ball being smaller *and* on a longer table is handled by the same arithmetic as everything else.
  - **`DailyGame::is_pool()` is the roster's one answer to "is this a cue game"**, and `DailyMatchDetail::pool()` / `pool_mut()` are the detail's. Every site asks one of those rather than spelling the variants out: a hand-written list in front of the input layer that misses one variant leaves that game's board completely dead, since the gate fronts *every* pool key and *every* pool click (no aim, no target, no stroke). A roster variant left out of a list that fronts an input layer is not a missing feature, it is an unplayable game.
  - **A snooker frame opens with the cue ball in hand in the D**, which is a rule rather than a nicety: where you break from decides what the pack does. `opening_placement` starts from a spot *inside* the zone on offer (`rack::d_spot` for the D), because `free_spot` walks outward from where it is pointed rather than hunting the table.
  - **Play-again rides on `Shot.play_again`** rather than a second move channel: it *is* the move (it takes the turn, joins the history and resets the clock), it just never reaches the simulator. `y` sends it, and `apply_shot` refuses it unless the ruleset is snooker *and* the last shot actually fouled. Both halves of that gate are load-bearing: in eight-ball and nine-ball the rule does not exist, and taking it would throw away the ball in hand the player was just awarded. Handing a shot back re-grants `BallInHand::TheD` whenever `must_place()`, which is also the real rule: clearing `ball_in_hand` while the cue ball sits in the pocket (after an in-off or any scratch) leaves the offender unable to place or shoot, and the match stuck until the 24h clock forfeits them.
  - **The free ball is decided on the table the incoming player will face, not the one the simulator left** (`DailyPoolState::table_after`). The rules layer cannot decide it alone (it never sees geometry), so the split is explicit: a foul returns `free_ball_if_snookered` and the caller answers the other half. The ordering is the whole point and it is easy to get backwards: `balls_to_spot` goes back up *before* `is_snookered` walks the table, or every colour the same foul is about to re-spot is missing from it, which is both an obstruction that is not counted and, in the colours-only phase, the wrong ball taken as the ball on. `table_after` takes the settled rack by value so there is no unspotted rack left in scope to ask by mistake.
  - **Only reds go down in multiples, and the ball on is one ball.** In the on-a-colour phase `legal_targets` returns *every* colour, which is the list of balls you may legally hit, not the ball that is on. Reading it as the latter scores two colours potted in one stroke as a legal break-building shot, and bills every foul at seven for as long as the black is up. The nominated colour (`outcome.first_contact`, per the module's inferred-nomination model) is the ball on there; the table decides it everywhere else.
  - **A snooker frame is called on points only on the black**: with the black alone on the table and more than seven in it, `apply_shot` ends the frame (`rules_snooker::only_black_left` + `out_of_reach`), which is the real rule. Anywhere earlier the player behind plays for snookers: calling any frame where the lead exceeds the points left was tried and rejected, because snooker attempts are the fun of a frame's end. The info panel says it in words: `pool_ui::snooker_scoreboard` (`break 23 · lead 18 · 51 left`, break kept as `DailyPoolState::current_break`, added on `Turn::Keep`, zeroed on a pass, hand-back or put-back) and `snookers_needed` (`weslin needs 2 snookers`, counted to *win*, since a level frame is a draw here rather than a re-spotted black; each snooker worth max(4, lowest colour on)). Snooker drops the blank row under the title to fit both lines in `INFO_ROWS`.
  - **Foul and a miss**: the miss call is a referee's judgement of intent, so it is approximated by its rule-book exceptions (`rules_snooker::is_miss`): any failure to hit the ball on first is a miss, *except* with only the black left or when either player needs snookers before or after the stroke. It terminates by itself (each miss pays the other side, so a serial misser soon needs snookers and stops being called); no three-miss rule. When called, `DailyPoolState::miss` keeps a `MissReplay` (the rack as struck, post-placement, and the offender's `on_colour` / `free_ball` / `ball_in_hand`). The fouled player's three choices: play from here, make them play from where it lies (`play_again`), put the balls back (`Shot.put_back`, a move that never reaches the simulator, like the hand-back).
  - **The choice is a dialog that must be answered** (`pool_ui::draw_foul_dialog`, `pool_draft::FoulChoice` / `FoulDialog`), not a legend entry: most players here do not know snooker has the rule, and `y b on a foul` taught nobody. It opens over the table whenever `may_return` and this player has not chosen (`PoolDraft::foul_dialog_open`; "play from here" is local and keyed by `move_count`, carried across reloads by `adopt`), says what happened, what it paid (`DailyPoolState::last_penalty`), the free ball if any, and what each choice does in words. While it is up it owns the keyboard (`pool_input::foul_dialog_key`: digits, `j`/`k`/arrows + Enter, `y`/`b` kept as shortcuts; `v`, `r`/`R`, `X` and leaving fall through, since watching the foul again is a fair thing to want first) and the mouse (a click on a choice picks it, everything else is swallowed; geometry in `PoolDetail::foul_hit`). The status line says `their foul: choose how to go on` (`Prompt::Fouled`).
  - **Bar rules, not league rules.** Only the eight is called (calling every shot is a whole extra input step per turn); the eight on the break is spotted rather than won or lost on; a scratch on the break gives the kitchen. Nine-ball leaves out push-out and the three-foul rule, both additive later. Results are their own `DailyResult` variants because "eight potted" over a loss would read as a win: `eight_potted`, `early_eight`, `nine_potted`, and snooker's `frame_won` (which names no ball: a frame is won on points, and the last black is just the last ball). Both display maps, `state::result_phrase` and `board_ui::result_banner`, are exhaustive over `DailyResult`, so a new result without its wording is a build error.
  - **Every shot is counted where it ends** (`play_pool_shot_task`, `metrics::record_pool_shot`, `PoolShotOutcome`). One counter, `late_ssh_pool_shots_total{outcome}`, with `settled` as the denominator for the other two: `rejected` is a refused shot (illegal stroke, out of turn, superseded), so a little is players and clients disagreeing and a rising line is a desync. `truncated` is the one to alert on and the reason the counter exists: `sim`'s `MAX_STEPS` / `MAX_TIME` guard rails turn a physics bug into a cut-short shot rather than a hung session, but the half-played rack is still written as the authoritative match, so without a count the guard rail trips in silence and the only symptom is a table that settled somewhere strange. It should be flat zero. The task is the one place every way a shot can end is named, per the errors-handled-centrally rule.
  - **`DAILY_WIN_MIN_MOVES` (5) can refuse an honest win**: a nine-ball golden break pays nothing. Accepted: it is the same gate every roster game lives under.

## Files

| File | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `pool.rs` | Pool match state for both games: `DailyPoolState` (persisted `state` JSON: ruleset, table preset name, seats, rack seed, current rack, the pre-shot rack, and the `Shot` history), `apply_shot` (placement -> strike -> simulate -> judge -> `table_after` -> fold the ruling back), `table_after` (re-spots, *then* asks whether the incoming player is snookered), `last_shot_sim` / `last_timeline` (the inputs for animating the last shot, and the simulation itself), `visit_start` / `rewound` / `replay` / `ReplayError` (where the last visit began, the opening rack rebuilt from the seed, and the history played forward through the ruleset for the `R` replay, refused rather than played when it no longer arrives at what is stored), `opening_ball_in_hand` (every game breaks from in hand), `spec`/`geometry` (preset lookup, an error rather than a fallback). Rules and physics are not here: they are `app/games/pool_core`. |
| `pool_draft.rs` | Everything pool keeps per-session that is not the persisted match: `PoolDraft` (the shot being composed and its gesture arithmetic), `PoolPlayback` and the `queue` behind it (a replayed visit is several shots), `PoolDetail` (incl. `new`, `is_busy`, `withholds_result`, and `frames_before_shot`, the rack the board holds while a shot is being re-simulated), `AimGear`, `PoolCueHit`, `PoolAimShare`'s throttle (`should_share_aim`), and every tuning constant. The rule that keeps it out of `state.rs`: if a function takes `(&mut PoolDraft, &DailyPoolState)` and touches no `DailyState` field, it lives here, not in the shared state. **Its last section is the board's pool seam** (`start_pool_playback`, `poll_pool_timeline`, `drive_pool_playback`, `pool_is_animating`, `start_pool_replay` and its `ReplaySpan`, `PoolFinishHold`, `pool_defers_finish`): pool is the only daily game that animates, so it is the only one that needs a physics worker, a playback queue and a reason to sit on news, and all of it reads and writes `DailyBoardState`. It lives here anyway because *every field it touches is a pool field*: the rule is about who owns the code, not which struct the bytes sit in. That is why this file imports from `state.rs` while `state.rs` imports from it; `state.rs` keeps the fields, one delegate apiece and the `MatchFinished` arm. |
| `pool_input.rs` | Pool's key map and mouse handling, dispatched from `board_input.rs` with one call per event kind: the input twin of `pool_ui.rs`. Gates only where it can: what a click on the cloth *means* is `PoolDraft::click_table`, and what one on the cue panel means is read off the `PanelHit` the panel handed back. Owns its own `is_pool_board` gate, asked of `DailyMatchDetail::pool()`. The two things it cannot do through the pub surface stay behind `DailyState` methods: sending a shot (`pool_send`, needs the service handle) and the aim broadcast (`pool_publish_aim`, needs the throttle fields). |
| `pool_ui.rs` | Pool board renderer: half-block table on the left, info panel + cue panel + readouts + key legend stacked on the right (`column_split`), the mouse hint on the status line (`shooter_spans`), `draw_too_small` under 112x30. Mid-shot it draws the sampled timeline instead of the settled rack and drops the aiming marks; while one is still being re-simulated it draws the rack from *before* it and says nothing about the result (`rolling`, `DailyBoardState::pool_shot_pending`). Records `target_geometry` (the table rect) and `cue_geometry` (the cue ball's drawn face) so a click becomes either a spot on the cloth or a tip placement; `table_point_at` inverts the render's own `View`, which is why `View::fit_area` exists. |
