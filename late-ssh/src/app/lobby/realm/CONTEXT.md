# Realm Context

## Metadata
- Scope: `late-ssh/src/app/lobby/realm` — multiplayer (2-10) simultaneous-turn territory conquest on real Earth; plus persistence in `late-core/src/models/realm_game.rs`, migrations 192/193/194, the map generator `tools/realm-mapgen`, and the committed assets `late-ssh/assets/realm/`.
- Domain: long-lived (weeks) DB-durable games. Each player gets N action points per *realm day*; an action (claim a free territory / attack an owned one) **resolves the instant it is taken** and everyone sees it on their next snapshot. Races are first-come-first-served through the row CAS. A realm day rolls over at the game's own `reset_hour_utc`, picked at creation, and only governs the budget and the idle clock. Win = conquering every rival (or, the same finish from the other end, holding every territory). Unclaimed land does not keep a realm open: mopping up ground nobody is contesting is bookkeeping, not conquest.

## 1. Shape

Three-layer split, daily-domain style (no live actor per game, load-validate-persist, everything survives restarts):

- **Rules as data.** `rulesets.rs::RULESETS` is the roster; every mechanic knob (min/max players, actions/day tiers by player count, kick days, probability constants, distance decay) is a `RealmRuleset` field. The **map is not**: it is picked per game from `map::MAPS` and frozen into the snapshot beside the rules, because the same rules on another world is another game. `map::MAPS` holds two entries — `earth` (fixed, from Natural Earth data) and `generated` (drawn per game; see §2a). A game freezes a serde `RealmRulesetSnapshot` into its state at creation, so editing a ruleset never mutates running games. New rulesets = new array entries, never new branches. Fields added since the first landing carry `#[serde(default)]` so games frozen before them still parse.
- **Instant engine.** `resolver.rs` owns the persisted state shape (`RealmGameState` in `realm_games.state` JSONB) and the pure `apply_action(state, map, actor, action, day, seed)`. One action = one point = one roll, applied on the spot. Seed = `blake3(game_id ‖ day ‖ revision ‖ actor ‖ target)`; RNG is a local SplitMix64 (portable forever). Because the revision is in the seed, a retry after a lost CAS is a genuinely new roll against the world as it actually is. Illegal actions (`ActionRejected`) are **refused**, not fizzled: nothing is spent, nothing is logged, the player is told and picks again. `sweep_idle` is the only other engine entry point: it dissolves a realm that every living player has been quiet in for `REALM_ABANDON_DAYS` (5, flat) and re-checks the end condition.
- **Attacking is the hard move.** Claiming free land is cheap; taking held land is not, and it gets harder the bigger the holder is: the strength ratio is raised to `attack_strength_exponent` (1.6), so a sprawling empire is a genuinely bad target and a cornered player is a soft one. On top of that an **opening truce** protects the early game — `attack_grace_days` (1) forbids every attack, `distant_attack_grace_days` (3) forbids attacks past your own border — both counted from `state.start_day`, both ruleset data. Claims are never blocked: the truce is there so players develop before anyone can be knocked out. `RealmGameState::attack_window(day)` is the single answer to "what is allowed right now", used by the engine, the rail, and the target table alike.
- **Routes are per landmass** (`Territory.landmasses` / `land_links`, built by `map::compute_landmasses` from the grid). A border only helps you if you are standing on the same landmass as the border: France borders both Brazil and Spain (French Guiana is France), and without this the atlas is a dry road from South America to Europe — which is exactly the bug that shipped, showing Europe as "on your border" from Brazil at full odds and letting a landlocked player walk off their continent. `ReachIndex` keys its Dijkstra on `(territory, landmass)`; holding a multi-part country means standing on each of its landmasses, which is right — you hold ground on both. Only landmasses a territory has a *border* on are kept (Earth is tens of thousands of islets; carrying them all would blow up the node set for nothing, and anything reached from a lone islet is reached by sea anyway).
- **No route is not a cheap route.** `RouteMode::None` / `Reach::UNREACHABLE` / `ActionRejected::NoRoute`: another landmass with nothing to sail from or nothing to land on is refused, not priced. The old fallback handed it `Reach::ADJACENT` — cost 1.0, "on your border", full odds — which is where that bug lived. The fallback now only covers a player holding nothing at all.
- **The newcomer shield scales with the world.** `RealmPlayer.joined_claimed` (percent of the map already taken, frozen at join) plus `newcomer_shield_max_days` (4 on standard, pace-scaled) stretch the `New` phase: the plain `attack_grace_days` for somebody who was here at the start, up to that plus the max at the join door (`join_max_claimed`, 50%), pro rata between. `state.shield_days(user)` is the one answer; the rampup window keeps its own length and moves along behind it. Serde-default 0 means games frozen before this behave exactly as they did. In the sims a latecomer now comes out of protection holding ~20-28 territories instead of the 1 they spawned on — what happens to them afterwards is endgame pressure, not arrival handicap.
- **Spawns prefer a land border, then free ground next to it** (`svc::spawn_player`). A lone island is a fine place to play from and a hopeless place to start from once sea crossings are real, and a spawn walled in by other people's borders has nothing to spend its shield on; both preferences fall back when nothing else is free.
- **Reach is what a route costs, not how many steps it has** (`ReachIndex`). Two ways to arrive, cheaper one wins:
  - *Overland*: Dijkstra from everything you hold, charging for each territory the path passes **through** — your own land is free, empty land costs `free_transit_cost` (1), and a rival's costs `enemy_transit_cost` (3). Marching past someone is the expensive way round, which is what makes a border worth holding. The target itself is never charged; you are taking it, not crossing it.
  - *By sea*: from a coastal holding, `km / sea_km_per_cost` (2000km per point). A strait is nearly free, an ocean is not. The target must have a coast — an inland country cannot be taken off a boat — and whether an inland *player* may sail at all is the `landlocked_sea_attack` option.
  - Odds pay `distance_decay^(cost−1)`, continuous, so a border move (cost 1) pays nothing and the sea's fractional costs work properly. The old `stranded` bonus is gone: an island player now just has sea costs like everyone else, which is what that special case was standing in for.
- **Fortifications** (`OPTION_FORTIFY`, on by default). `f` — and only `f` — spends a point on land you hold to raise it a level; the attack key refuses your own ground with a nudge rather than quietly digging, because a point spent on walls is a point not spent taking ground and nobody should spend one by reaching for the wrong key. It raises it a level, to `fort_max_level` (5); level one is simply holding the place and is never stored. Each level past the first multiplies an attacker's odds by `fort_defence_per_level` (0.88) and adds `fort_transit_per_level` (0.4) to the cost of marching past — so a fortified pass is worth going *around*, which the routing will do by itself. Taking a territory levels its walls, so offence is rewarded with a weak position and a frontier never sets. Walls fade with their holder's dormancy (the decay multiplier walks the bonus back towards nothing), because an abandoned fortress stalling a realm for everyone else is exactly the shape we spent the anti-quitting work avoiding.
  - **The economy is deliberately unfavourable**: four points of wall buy fewer than four points of attack (asserted in `resolver_test`), because a realm is won by taking ground. Digging in is for the pass you mean to keep or a day with nothing better. `simulation_test::digging_in_is_not_a_way_to_win` plays two pure turtles in a five-player game and requires that they never win and that the realm still ends — that test is the balance, not the arithmetic above it.
  - Shown everywhere, to everyone: the map draws each level as a step on that player's colour ladder (`state::PLAYER_PALETTES`, five shades, pale = open ground, dark = walled up), so solid-looking means solid and it is the only signal that survives a one-pixel country, the target table has a `walls` bar column and the rail card draws the same bars (`map_ui::fort_bar`, shared so the two cannot drift) plus what they cost an attacker, the standings total each player's walls so a turtle is visible, and the log records each dig.
- **Options** (`rulesets::OPTIONS`, chosen per game, stored in the snapshot): `long_range_strikes` (on by default) and `landlocked_sea_attack` (off). They are data, so adding one is an array entry plus a place that reads it; a game frozen before an option existed takes the roster default rather than a rule nothing enforces. With long-range **off**, a move must stay inside `short_reach_cost` (2) — and that cap **lifts entirely for a player with nothing in range** (`anything_within`), so a short-reach game can never box someone into having no move.
- **`RealmState::tick()` returns `RealmTick { banner, changed }`**, and `changed` is the load-bearing half. A realm action resolves in a service task, so the event comes back on one frame and the reloaded board on a later one; reporting only the banner left the second frame waiting for the next keypress (claim a territory, be told you have it, watch the map keep showing it as somebody else's until you move). `changed` is set by: a new snapshot, any event, a board or log load landing, and the muster countdown crossing a second — the one thing on screen that changes with nothing arriving, so it asks for its own frames.
- **Service.** `svc.rs::RealmService`, process-global (constructed in `main.rs` beside `DailyService`): watch snapshot + broadcast events + fire-and-forget mutator tasks + the 60s sweeper. `act()` is the hot path: load, roll, persist under the row CAS, archive the log line, emit `ActionResolved` (targeted, the actor's banner) and `GameChanged` (everyone else reloads). The sweeper no longer resolves anything — it rolls each game's day over at that game's `reset_hour_utc` (archiving the day it closes) and dissolves realms nobody has touched in five days.

## 2. Persistence (late-core)

- `realm_games` (migration 192, + `reset_hour_utc` in 195, + `name` in 196): `name` is stored exactly as tidied (`sanitize_name`: control characters flattened, whitespace collapsed, 32 chars max) and stays empty when the creator typed nothing — the read side (`game_name`) fills in "<creator>'s realm", so a rename would never have to rewrite rows. Otherwise: status open/active/finished/cancelled, `ruleset_id`, `reset_hour_utc`, `last_resolved_day` (the last realm day the sweeper closed out — DB-durable, never an in-process timer), state JSONB with top-level `revision` CAS (`stored_revision_eq_sql`, daily_match pattern). Now written by every action too, which is exactly what makes the first-come-first-served race honest.
- `realm_actions` (migration 193) is **gone** (dropped by migration 195). With instant resolution there is no queue to hide, and every action writes the game row anyway.
- `realm_days` (193, + `board` in 198): the per-day log archive. A day's row is rewritten as the day is played (`archive_day` upserts), so history survives even if the process dies mid-day; the game row keeps only the current day in `state.last_day`. Paged by the log view.
- `realm_game.rs` methods: `create_game`, `count_active_memberships` (JSONB `@>` on the players array; cap `REALM_MAX_ACTIVE_GAMES = 20`), `list_open/active/finished_recent(30d)/active_for_sweep`, `update_state_cas`, `start_cas`, `advance_day_cas`, `finish_cas`, `cancel`, `archive_day`, `load_day_logs`. The sweep list is unfiltered on purpose: "which day is it" is per row now, so it is decided in Rust.

## 2a. Generated worlds (`mapgen.rs`)

- **A generated map is a recipe, not a payload.** The game stores a `GeneratedMapSpec` — seed, continents, islands, territories, and the list of names — in `ruleset.map_spec`, and `mapgen::generate` rebuilds the identical world from it every time. A 400-territory world is ~1 MB of cells; what goes in JSONB is ~40 lines of JSON. Names are the one part that is *stored* rather than derived, because they may come from the AI service and that is not a pure function of the seed.
- **The seed is the server's.** `svc::rand_seed()` draws it at creation; the client sends only the three counts. A client-chosen seed would let a creator re-roll until their own spawn corner looked good.
- **The globe is Earth's globe.** Same equirectangular projection, same sphere, land held at ~29% of it whatever the parameters. So `sea_hop_km`, the distance decay and every other km-tuned ruleset constant mean the same thing on a generated world as on the real one — the parameters change the shape of a game, never how much world there is. Grid is 1024×512 (a quarter of Earth's linear resolution; invented coastlines do not need survey detail), mips built the same way.
- **How it is drawn.** Landmass centres by best-of-N farthest-point sampling (latitudes drawn in sine space, nothing past 72°); each mass grown by a cheapest-first flood over a shape field, so it is connected by construction however wild the field gets. The field is three layers, because any one alone reads as a blob: distance to the nearest of 2-4 **nuclei** strung along a random axis (a continent gets a long side and a waist instead of a middle), divided by a radius breathing with four **angular harmonics** (gulfs and headlands), all measured at a **domain-warped** coordinate — two low-frequency noise fields bend the ground before it is measured, which is what folds a smooth arc into bays, spits and the occasional isthmus — with a further fractal-noise term on the radius for detail down to cell scale. `continents_are_not_discs` pins it with the isoperimetric quotient (`4πA/P²`: 1.0 for a circle, ~0.6 for the first cut of this generator, 0.11-0.19 now, 0.014 for Earth's Afro-Eurasia on its four-times-finer grid); territories allocated per mass by *actual* area, then cut by multi-source Dijkstra from scattered seeds with per-seed speed drawn log-normal — which is what gives a world a few sprawls and many small countries. Area is summed from per-row cell areas (cos-latitude), population is area × a density drawn over three orders of magnitude.
- **Names.** `assets/realm/invented_names.txt` is a built-in pool of 1000 invented names; `svc::invent_names` asks the AI service first (batches of `NAME_BATCH` = 80, `NAMING_BUDGET` = 20s, one of `NAME_FLAVOURS` picked by seed so two worlds do not read as the same continent) and tops up from the pool for anything missing, rejected, or repeated. **The pool is not a degraded mode**: it is what tests and any keyless box always use, and a pool-named world is a normal world.
- **Handles, not statics.** Because a generated map is built at runtime, `map_by_id` and `map_handle` return `Arc<RealmMap>` and `RealmMap.id`/`display_name` are `String`. `map_handle(id, spec)` is the single lookup: fixed ids hit the `LazyLock` statics, `generated` hits `mapgen::map_for_spec`, which caches up to `MAP_CACHE_MAX` (24) built worlds keyed by spec hash and builds outside the lock.

## 3. Lifecycle & rules (standard ruleset)

- **A realm musters for `REALM_MUSTER` (2 minutes) before anyone can act.** Creating one announces it — a banner to every session and a lounge line from the creator (`ActivityKind::RealmForming`) — and for those two minutes the realm is visible and joinable but frozen: map, spawn and roster all readable, every action refused with the countdown. It closed the creator's edge: they used to be able to spend a whole day's points before anybody else knew the game existed. The freeze is the **one rule the engine does not hold**: `resolver.rs` has no clock at all (it is day-quantised by design), so threading a wall-clock instant through `apply_action` and every simulation playout to enforce something that expires in 120 seconds would be the wrong trade. It lives in `svc::act_at` and, for the pre-flight refusal, `state::act_on`/`fortify_on`. `svc::muster_left` is the single answer; there is no new column, since `realm_games.created` already says when the clock started. `act_at(now)` exists so tests need not wait two real minutes (`sweep(now)` precedent); tests that are not about the muster wind `created` back an hour (`svc_test::open_the_doors`).
- **There is no starting gun.** Creating a realm makes it live: the creator spawns on a random free territory and can act at once (once the muster is out). Everyone else joins a war already in progress, spawning on arrival — no waiting room, no roster to assemble, no synchronised start. `join_game` accepts while the roster has room and the map is under `join_max_claimed` (50%); past that a newcomer would only be food, so the doors close (`RealmGameState::joinable`, surfaced as `RealmGameItem::joinable`/`claimed`).
- **Protection is per player, not per game** (`PlayerPhase`, counted from each player's own `joined_day`): **new** for `attack_grace_days` (1) — free land only, and untouchable; **ramping up** until `distant_attack_grace_days` (3) — neighbours only, and only neighbours may come for them; then **involved**, where anything goes both ways. `attack_allowed(actor, defender, hops, day)` is the single check, and it refuses in both directions, so arriving on day forty buys the same footing the founders had.
- **The daily budget follows the involved**, not the roster (`daily_base` → `involved_count`): a realm whose players are all still finding their feet pays the most generous tier, which is right when one or two people are alone on a whole world.
- **The day turning over is announced twice** (`svc::day_rolled_events`). Every player on the roster gets `RealmEvent::DayRolled` — which carries the game's *name*, because the session raises it as a banner wherever they are in the app ("The Realm calls you! (name)"), not only with the board open. And the lounge ticker gets one line, `ActivityKind::RealmCalls` → "realm <name> calls its players", attributed to no user because the summons is the game's. That line is gated on more than one living player: a realm somebody made and sat in alone would otherwise post about nothing, daily, for as long as the row lives. The repeat key is `realm-calls:<game>:<day>`, so a rollover announced twice posts once.
- **Reset hour** (`realm_games.reset_hour_utc`, 0-23): when this game's points come back, every day. Picked at creation so a US game and an EU game can both refill at a civilised local hour instead of a global midnight. Every surface writes it in both clocks (`svc::reset_hour_label`, `RealmState::viewer_tz`, mirrored from `App` like the chat pane's zone).
- Actions/day scale with player count at start: 2-4 → 7, 5-7 → 5, 8-10 → 4 (`actions_per_day_tiers`). A point is spent on the attempt, won or lost; a *refused* action costs nothing.
- Probability (pure fn `action_probability`): territory weight `w = (ln pop + ln area normalized)/2`; strength `S = 1 + Σ held weights`; claim `0.90 − 0.25w`; attack `0.50·(2S_att/(S_att+S_def)) − 0.15w`; then `× distance_decay^(hops−1)`, `× stranded_mult` when boxed in; clamp to [0.05, 0.95] on your border, [0.01, 0.95] beyond it. The UI's projected % is the same function on the same state — it is the real number, it can just go stale the moment someone else acts.
- Exits: voluntary leave and inactivity kick free the territories (open-game creator leaving cancels the game); losing your last territory = **Eliminated**, stays spectator. One alive player left (every rival conquered) finishes the game, as does someone owning every territory — the same finish reached from either end. Free land left on the map does not hold it open. Zero alive dissolves it (no winner, no payouts), as does a realm every living player has been quiet in for `REALM_ABANDON_DAYS`.
- **Pace** (`rulesets::PACES`, picked at creation, frozen into the snapshot by `RealmRulesetSnapshot::at_pace`). Orthogonal to the ruleset: the rules say how a conquest works, the pace says how long it takes. `action_scale` multiplies the daily points, `day_scale` multiplies every window measured in days (opening protection, the week a win takes, dormancy, banking), and `pot_scale` multiplies the prize. Floors keep a blitz playable — a win you could reach in a day, or an opening with no protection, would be a different game rather than a faster one. Measured on five mixed policies: **epic 158 days, slow 68, normal 39, fast 17, blitz 12** (`simulation_test.rs`). `slow` is the rules exactly as written, and `normal` is the reference tempo whose pot is the plain per-player rate.
- **A finished realm stays in the Lobby for `REALM_RESULTS_HOURS` (24)**, so everyone gets to read the result, then drops out of the listing. The row is never deleted: the history and the archived day logs outlive the listing.
- **Ranked payouts** (migration 194, rescaled by 197). The pot is computed per game: `REALM_POT_PER_PLAYER` (3000) × `paying_players` — everyone who cleared `min_payout_active_days`, floored at two. Counting the roster instead would let a colluder walk alts in to swell the prize without taking a turn, which matters now that arrivals are open all game; an honest latecomer who stays grows the pot, a body thrown in to pad it does not. So: so a duel is worth 6k and a ten-player war 30k — placing in a crowded game is the harder feat and pays like it. Multiplied by the pace's `pot_scale` (epic 3.0 → blitz 0.25) and capped at `REALM_POT_MAX` (60k, because ten players at the epic tempo would otherwise compute a prize bigger than anything else on the platform pays). Split by tier (`payout_plan`): ≤3 winner takes all; 4-6 → 70/30; 7+ → 60/25/15, rounded to the nearest 50. That puts a realm win in the door-game milestone class (Green Dragon 10k, A Dark Room 15-20k) instead of below a daily Asterion escape (4000), where the old flat 2000 sat. The six `reward_templates` rows still own the claim policy (`per_event`, one payment per rank per game) and the game/payout_kind identity; `reward_chips` on them is the floor of each tier, and `credit_per_event_reward_template_amount` passes the computed figure. Ledger reason is `ChipMove::RealmConquest` (`realm_conquest`), which counts as earnings on the Top Chips board.
- **You can withdraw on your first day, and not after.** While you are `New` nothing has happened to you and nothing has happened because of you, so leaving is free; past that you are committed, because quitting used to hand the game to whoever was left. The last player in a realm may always pack the whole thing up (`RealmGame::cancel`), since there is nobody to hand it to. Stopping otherwise is something you do by not playing: your land stays yours and stays takeable.
- **Winning takes a week of play, against real rivals.** `can_win` wants `min_players` on the roster (a realm sat in alone is not a conquest) and `min_active_days_to_win` (7) *distinct days the player acted*, tracked as `RealmPlayer::active_days` — elapsed days would be farmable by waiting. Eliminating everyone sooner does not end the game (`check_end` returns `Ongoing`); you hold the field until the days are in. Both win conditions go through the same bar, which never bites on Earth (a conquest there is weeks of work) but stops a 12-territory map being swept in an afternoon with an obliging friend.
- **Missed days bank, on a ladder** (`standing`, `bank_rate`): miss one and it banks at 75%, a few (to `bank_missed_cutoff` = 3) at 50%, past that a missed day banks nothing. The bank is capped at `bank_cap_days` (3) days' worth, which is what stops a fortnight away from becoming one flattening blitz. The day's allowance is **frozen at the first action of the day** (`RealmPlayer::actions_allowance`) — the gap it is computed from closes the moment you act, so without freezing it the bank would evaporate on the first spend.
- **An abandoned empire crumbles** (`decay_multiplier`): after `dormancy_grace_days` (3) quiet days a player's strength falls `decay_per_day` (10%) per further day, floored at `decay_floor` (0.40). Their land defends with less than its size suggests, so quiet players get carved up rather than freezing the game — and nobody is removed, which is what keeps "go quiet" from laundering into an exit. `effective_strength` is what the odds use; `strength` stays the raw figure.
- **The sweeper no longer kicks.** It rolls days over, and dissolves a game only when *every* living player has been quiet for `resolver::REALM_ABANDON_DAYS` (5 days, flat — not scaled by ruleset or pace, because what it measures is whether anybody still comes back). A player who never acted counts from their join day, so a realm nobody ever played clears itself too. That path pays nothing, so it cannot be farmed. The per-user cap (`REALM_MAX_ACTIVE_GAMES`, 20) leans on this: it exists so one person cannot fill the Lobby, not to ration play.
- **A game has to have been played to pay** (`payout_earned`: the winner cleared the week and holds at least `min_payout_territories`). Per player, `payout_eligible` also wants `min_payout_active_days` (3) of their own — the podium means "played and placed", not "was in the roster".
- Ranking (`final_ranking`): winner, then later exit day > exit territory count > seeded jitter; **leavers and kicked players are payout-ineligible** and the next eligible player moves up.
- Activity: ONE #lounge line per game, on conquest only (`ActivityGame::Realm` → `GameWon`, approved in `filter.rs`). Nothing on create/join/start/act.

## 4. Map

> The atlas itself lives in `app::common::worldmap` (`data` = territories,
> grids, mips, Earth; `view` = viewport + painter). It was lifted out of here
> when `/map` wanted the same Earth. `lobby/realm/map.rs` is now just

- `common::worldmap::data`: `WorldMap { territories, grid, mips }`. Two maps: `testmap` (12 hand-built territories; engine tests) and `earth` (242 Natural Earth countries; the standard ruleset). Territory `weight` is precomputed per map from that map's own pop/area maxima.
- Earth assets are COMMITTED and embedded: `earth_territories.json` (~30KB; id/iso/name/POP_EST/area/neighbors/center) and `earth_grid.bin` (~200KB RLE; header `RLM1 | u16 w=4096 | u16 h=2048 | u8 flags | u16 count`, body `(u16 run, u16 cell)`, `0xFFFF` water; `flags` bit0 reserves a future appended terrain plane — readers decode exactly w·h cells and ignore trailing bytes). ≈10km/cell at the equator. `mips` are majority-vote halvings (land beats water on ties) down to ≤160 cells wide, built at load for zoom-out.
- Generator: `tools/realm-mapgen` (workspace bin; dev downloads Natural Earth 50m admin-0 GeoJSON, not committed — see the bin's header for the exact commands). `--width` picks the grid resolution (default 4096 ≈ 10km/cell; `--width 8192` halves that at roughly 4x the asset size). The reader takes w/h from the asset header, so a finer map needs no code change. Scanline rasterization, raster-derived land adjacency (75 island countries are jump-only), zero-cell microstates force-painted one cell, `--check` validates round-trip + adjacency symmetry. Multi-part countries are ONE territory with unioned neighbors (France borders Brazil, because French Guiana does). That is the asset's model and it stays — but it is **not** a route: land adjacency is paired with the landmass it is on (`Territory.land_links`), so crossing France does not walk you from South America to Europe. See §3.

## 4a. History (the finished realm, walked day by day)

- **Every day keeps the board it closed on.** `realm_days.board` (migration
  198) holds a `DayBoard` — the roster, `(territory, slot)` pairs and the
  walls — written once at the rollover, which is the moment worth keeping: the
  map as it stood just before everyone's points came back. Written again at
  the finish, onto the day the game ended on, since there is no rollover left
  to carry it.
- **The snapshot carries its own roster.** A player who withdraws is *removed*
  from `RealmGameState::players`, so an index into the live roster means
  somebody else afterwards. `DayBoard::players` (`DayPlayer { user_id,
  username, color }`) makes a day self-describing: it replays in the colours
  it was played in, and it can still name somebody the game has forgotten.
  Territories point at slots in *that* list, because a uuid is 36 bytes and
  there are up to 242 a day; free land is absent rather than recorded.
- **`archive_day` takes `Option<&board>`** and the upsert does
  `board = COALESCE(EXCLUDED.board, realm_days.board)`. The day row is
  rewritten on every action, so a live mid-day write must never replace the
  closing snapshot with a half-played one.
- **Three log entries were missing and now are not**: `Spawned` (arrivals were
  never logged at all — a game's opening positions were nobody's doing),
  `Left` with the ground it freed, and `freed` on `Kicked` (which only ever
  counted). Without these the map could not be replayed from the log however
  complete the actions looked. `RealmGameState::log` is `pub(super)` for
  exactly these two service-side entries.
- **The view** is `history_ui.rs`: `RealmView::History` in the Tab cycle.
  Its whole interface is the slider — `[`/`]` or `←`/`→` walk the days,
  `h`/`l` too, `g`/`G` jump to the ends — and there is deliberately **no pan
  or zoom**: the map always shows the whole world at the same frame, because
  walking the days is a comparison and two frames of a different picture do
  not compare. The track is drawn between literal `[` and `]` so the strip
  says how to move it without spending a line on saying so. The strip sits
  **above** the map and leads with the word — "history · 12 Sep · day 3 of
  45" — because this view and the live board draw the same world in the same
  colours, and what tells them apart must not be the last line on screen.
  Days show as dates; the stored number is days since the epoch, which is
  right to compute with and no use to read. It **opens on day one**: the last
  day's board is all but the live map, which is the one thing this view must
  not be taken for, while day one is a handful of spawns on an empty world.
  For "day 1 of N" to be true the whole archive has to be in — the pages are
  fourteen days each and the *first* page is the newest fourteen, so an
  unfinished run's oldest day is somewhere in the middle. `pull_history_archive`
  keeps asking for the next page from the frame loop while this view is open
  (driving it off "a page landed" missed the usual case, where the first page
  arrives while the board is still on the map), and until the run is in the
  strip says "reading the run in…" rather than calling its oldest known day
  the first. The log view still pages on demand, as it always did. It draws through a `HistoryFrame`
  gathered before drawing, which is what lets it be tested without a session.
  Days archived before boards existed have logs and no positions; they are
  skipped rather than drawn as an empty world, which would read as everybody
  losing everything that day.
- **No backfill, by nature.** Games finished before this have no positions to
  recover.

## 5. UI surfaces

- **Lobby modal section** (`realm/modal_ui.rs` — the row and both overlays live with realm, not in the Lobby's own `../modal_ui.rs`, which keeps the list they go in; shared widgets in `../modal_widgets.rs`. Input in `../modal_input.rs`): always-present `realm` section between live games and house tables — my games first, then joinable/watchable. Enter is seat-aware: join (confirm) / start (creator, confirm) / play / watch / view result; `x` leaves-disbands an OPEN game only; `n` opens the create overlay, which is **six steps** (`RealmCreateDraft`), seven when the world is generated: ruleset, map, *(the shape step — continents 0-10, islands 0-20, countries 50-400, `j`/`k` picks the number and `h`/`l` changes it, countries in tens; only reached when the generated world is picked, and Enter refuses a world with neither continent nor island)*, pace, the daily reset hour written in both the creator's clock and UTC, a name (typed, `REALM_NAME_MAX` = 32 chars; the name step owns printable input so `j`/`k` type rather than scroll), your colour, then the rule toggles. Joining runs its own one-step overlay (`RealmJoinDraft`) for the same colour choice, listing who already wears what and refusing to put the cursor on a taken one; the service settles it again (`svc::pick_color`) because two people can pick at once. Rows lead with the name, with the creator moved into the detail column. Esc peels a step before it closes. Confirm state is `LobbyState::confirm_realm`.
- **Watching** (`state::ViewerRole`): a realm can be opened by anyone — `w` on a Lobby row watches without joining (Enter on a joinable realm joins, which is why the watch key exists), and Enter on a closed or full one watches by default. The role is derived per board: `Playing` (in it and alive), `Fallen` (on the roster, out of the war), `Watching` (never in it). Read-only is enforced twice over — `RealmState::act_on` refuses before the round trip, and `apply_action` refuses again with `ActionRejected::NotPlaying` — so the presentation only has to be honest, not load-bearing. What changes: the header trades points and the refill countdown for the state of the world, the rail drops the points card and the odds (they are computed from land the viewer does not have), the target table drops the hops and odds columns and becomes an atlas, the standings say what they are looking at instead of "you hold nothing", and the footer stops offering keys that do nothing.
- **Selected rows are filled, not just marked.** The target table and the
  holdings list pad each row to the panel width and hang `theme::row_style`
  off the line, so the selection covers every column rather than stopping at
  the last word — six columns of a row is too much to tie together with a
  `▸` alone. `row_style` is the shared treatment (a fill where the palette has
  a paintable canvas, `REVERSED` where it does not), which is why the check
  compares only the part it contributes: cells keep their own fg and bold.
- **`Screen::Realm`** (outside the Tab cycle; entered only from the modal; `q`/Esc back to the modal): one screen, four Tab-cycled views (`RealmView`):
  - *Map* (`map_ui.rs`): half-block ▀ world render (2 px/terminal row). The viewport is **continuous**: `scale` is base-grid cells per drawn pixel (`MIN_SCALE` 0.25 magnifies to ~2.5km/px, the ceiling fits the whole world), `+`/`-` and the wheel multiply it by `ZOOM_STEP` (1.25), and `level_for` picks the finest mip that is not finer than the scale so small countries survive zooming out while the steps stay smooth. Pan with arrows or a left-button drag (a press that moves pans, one that does not selects — `drag_anchor`/`dragged`). **Territory outlines** are computed in screen space (does this pixel's territory differ from its neighbours?), so a border is one pixel wide at any zoom — and they are drawn only at `BORDER_MAX_SCALE` (6, ~60km/px) or finer: on a whole-world view most countries are a pixel or two wide, so outlining them is noise rather than information (owner feedback). A continent gone one owner's colour is still readable; the globe stays clean. `n`/`N` walks your own territories and frames each; `z` re-frames the selection; framing goes through `focus_request` because only the renderer knows the drawn area (it reads the territory's `bbox`, computed once at map build). Right rail: selected-territory card (owner, pop/area, live odds, hops out, or why the truce forbids it), action points + refill countdown + the zoom in km/px, the last few log lines, players.
  - *Targets* (`targets_ui.rs`): the map as a table — **your own territories first** (so it doubles as the atlas of what you hold), then everything you can act on, nearest first then best odds, with holder, hops, population and the real percentage (or `war Nd` / `near Nd` while the truce blocks it). `j`/`k` walk it (the cursor mirrors into the map selection, so Tab keeps your place), Enter/`a` acts, `g` shows the row on the map framed; Enter on your own land frames it too, since there is nothing to act on. This is the view for playing without squinting at a 4096-wide grid.
  - *Overview* (`overview_ui.rs`): standings with land, people, area, share of the world's population, **effective** strength (decay included) and days played per player, plus points left with anything banked, or days quiet and how far their grip has slipped. Then your own clock spelled out — days played against the bar, today's allowance and bank, what another missed day would bank, and how long before your own empire starts to crumble — the world totals, the pot, and the truce countdown while it lasts. Your holdings below are a cursor list: `j`/`k` walks them, `g`/Enter frames one on the map.
  - *Results* (`results_ui.rs`): the card that goes up when a realm ends — who took it and how, then every player with their land, days played, chips and how they went out, then what the pot paid. It raises itself once per session when a finished game loads (and on the `GameFinished` event, with a banner), any key puts it away, `r` calls it back. The table comes from `svc::final_table`, which is **the same function the payout walks** — a results screen that recomputed the ranking would eventually disagree with the ledger, and the ledger would be right.
  - *Log* (`log_ui.rs`): the live day from state + archived days paged from `realm_days` (auto-loads as you scroll).
  - `a` acts on the selection immediately (claim/attack auto-picked by ownership); `act_in_flight` blocks a second action until the result lands, so a double-tap cannot spend two points. `x` twice abandons an active game.
- **Session state** (`state.rs::RealmState` at `App::realm`): snapshot/event drains in `tick()` (result banners from `ActionResolved`, quiet reloads from `GameChanged`, a refill banner from `DayRolled`), board open/close with `return_screen`, oneshot row/log loads (daily board pattern), and `viewer_tz` mirrored from `App` for the hour labels.

## 6. Critical invariants

- An action is resolved exactly once, by the write that wins the row CAS. The retry after a lost CAS re-rolls against the reloaded state (the revision is in the seed) — it never replays the old roll.
- A *refused* action changes nothing: no point, no log line, no state write. Only rolls are history.
- The projected percentage on screen and the rolled percentage come from the same `action_probability` call on the same state. If they ever diverge, the game is lying to players.
- Days are DB integers derived from the game's own `reset_hour_utc` (`svc::realm_day`); never an in-process timer, and never a global midnight.
- The engine (`resolver.rs`, `map.rs`, `rulesets.rs`) stays pure — no db/tokio imports; `svc.rs` owns all IO.
- `state.revision` only grows; CAS losers reload.
- Ruleset snapshots are frozen at create; never resolve against the live `RULESETS` entry. New ruleset fields need `#[serde(default)]`.
- Asset format: readers must keep ignoring unknown trailing planes in `earth_grid.bin` so terrain can land later without a version break.
- `LogEntry::Fizzled` and `FizzleReason` are retired but must stay in the enum: archived days from the queue era still deserialize through them.

## 7. Tests

- **Playouts (`simulation_test.rs`, 7)**: whole games against the real engine, seeded so a failure is reproducible. Two, five and eight players with mixed policies (settler / warlord / cautious / absentee) run to a winner; every day checks the invariants that must never break (land held by the dead, a budget overspent or over its ceiling, decay past its floor, active days exceeding days elapsed, odds outside the ruleset's bounds, an exit without an exit day). Also: the join window staying open at least a week, a latecomer arriving at the last moment and still building something, an absentee surviving but falling behind, and end-to-end determinism. Observed with the current knobs: **2p resolves in ~47 days, 5p in ~60, 8p in ~79**; the map fills around day 26 (5p) to 37 (8p); the doors to newcomers close around day 8-9. Those numbers are printed by the tests (`--nocapture`), so a ruleset change that shifts the feel of the game shows up as a changed line rather than a silent drift.
- Engine (`resolver_test.rs`, 16): a point spent per attempt won or lost, budgets running out and refilling with the day, illegal actions refused for free, the first-come-first-served race, hop distance and its collapse in the odds, a long strike being legal at long odds, the island/stranded bonus, elimination and last-one-standing, idle kicks freeing land, day rollover handing back the finished log, seed determinism (and a retry re-rolling), ranking tie-breaks, the opening truce (no attacks at all, then borders only) and its countdown.
- Rules (`rulesets.rs` inline, 6): probability clamps and monotonicity, the distance collapse never passing 1%, the stranded bonus staying worse than a border move, a bigger defender beating the linear expectation, tier scaling, snapshot serde.
- Map (`map.rs` inline): testmap shape, earth asset consistency (adjacency symmetry, centers on own land, mips). Viewport maths lives in `map_ui.rs` inline tests: zoom range and step size, the sampled level never being coarser than the scale, bounding boxes framing their own land.
- Service (`svc_test.rs`, real `test_db`, 11): create/join/start lifecycle + auto-spawn + the persisted reset hour, capacity cap, an action resolving and landing in both the live log and the archive, the budget running out on the game's own hour, a stale target refused without cost, a distant strike allowed at long odds, the sweeper rolling and archiving a day (idempotently), idle kicks dissolving a game, leave→last-standing win + 2000-chip payout (leavers unpaid), open-game leave/creator-cancel, and the day/hour arithmetic.
- `cargo-nextest` is not installed on this machine, so the documented `make test-llm` filter fails; run the same scoped thing by hand (`make check-db`, then `cargo test -p late-ssh --lib realm` with `TEST_DATABASE_URL` set, then `make check-db-down`).
