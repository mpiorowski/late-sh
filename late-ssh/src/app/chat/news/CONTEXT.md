# News

Part of the chat domain (`../CONTEXT.md`, §11 Synthetic Entries).

- Backed by persisted `articles`.
- `ArticleService::process_url` extracts title/summary/image and stores an article. It posts nothing into chat: the #lounge live strip features the new article (below), and the right sidebar's Live panel lists it for an hour (`../../live/CONTEXT.md`), so a share can still be caught after the strip has moved on.
- **Three extraction paths, picked by URL shape in `do_process_url`.** YouTube (`is_youtube_url`): oEmbed pins title/author/thumbnail, then the AI writes only the summary against that verified identity, so the video can never be misidentified; an AI failure degrades to `youtube_fallback_summary`. X posts (`is_tweet_url`): **no AI at all**, see below. Everything else: `extract_via_ai`, Gemini with Google Search grounding, which works because those pages are server-rendered, indexed, and carry real `og:` tags.
- **X posts carry their own metadata, so nothing about them is guessed.** `extract_tweet` reads `publish.x.com/oembed` and parses the author, the post's own text, and the date straight out of the returned `<blockquote>` (oEmbed has no plain-text field for the text; `tweet_text_from_oembed_html` turns `<br>` into the author's line breaks, unwraps `<a>` to its text, and drops X's own `pic.twitter.com/...` media shortlinks). A post's words *are* the content, so there is nothing to research or summarize and the AI never sees the URL. This is not a preference: x.com serves no `og:` tags even to `Twitterbot`, and Search has next to nothing indexed against a bare status URL, so the AI path invented titles for these links. Only `/status/<id>` URLs take this path; a profile, search, or list URL has no post to resolve and stays on the generic AI path.
- oEmbed carries neither an image nor a sensitivity flag, so `fetch_tweet_media` gets both from **fxtwitter** (`api.fxtwitter.com/i/status/<id>`), the one third-party dependency in the pipeline, isolated in that single function. It sends an explicit `User-Agent` because fxtwitter answers `401` without one and `reqwest` sends none by default. Its `possibly_sensitive` is the **only NSFW gate on the X path**, so the lookup **fails closed**: any error, non-2xx status, or body without a post rejects the share with "X could not confirm this post is safe to share right now" rather than posting it unscreened. A successful lookup with no image still publishes, falling back to `procedural_ascii_art`. `metrics::record_news_x_media_lookup` labels `late_ssh_news_x_media_lookups_total` by the closed `XMediaLookup` outcome (`clean` / `sensitive` / `unavailable`); a run of `unavailable` is what an fxtwitter outage looks like.
- Publishing pays the sharer `NEWS_SHARE_REWARD_CHIPS` (500) as `ChipMove::NewsShared`. `Article::create_shared` (`late-core/src/models/article.rs`) is the only path a user-facing share may take, so the News composer and an RSS `s` share pay exactly the same. Chips are minted, not moved, and count toward Top Chips.
- The reward is capped at one per URL per user and at `NEWS_SHARE_MAX_PAID_PER_DAY` (3) paid shares per UTC day, and the `chip_ledger` row is what enforces both, keyed on `(user_id, url)` and counted by `created_at` date like pot tickets (hence `source_ref` holds the URL, not an article id; migration 163 indexes the lookup). The `articles` row cannot be the record of payment: deleting a story frees its URL, so paying on insert alone would let one player share, delete, and re-share the same link forever. `articles.url` is unique, so while a story is live only its first sharer was paid.
- A repeat or capped share still succeeds and still goes up on the live strip; it just mints nothing. `Article::create_shared` returns a closed `NewsShareReward` (`Paid` / `RepeatUrl` / `DailyCapReached`) that rides `ArticleEvent::Created` and `FeedEvent::EntryShared`, so `news::state::news_share_banner` says what the ledger did ("+500 chips" / "Already paid for this link" / "Today's 3 paid shares are used up") and `metrics::record_news_shared` labels `late_ssh_news_shares_total` by the same outcome. An RSS entry marked shared because its link was already in News carries `reward: None` and raises no second banner over "Already shared.".
- Insert, ledger lookup, and credit are one transaction under a `pg_advisory_xact_lock` keyed on `('news_share', user_id)`, the shape `GamePayout` and the pot use: a failed credit leaves no orphan article squatting on a globally unique URL, and two shares by one person landing together serialize, so the day cap is exact rather than read-then-write.
- Every snapshot article is a live strip candidate, stamped with its `created` (`news/live.rs`, `../../live/CONTEXT.md`): a share joins the strip's News lane, which goes ahead of everything else at the next handover, and stays up exactly five minutes. `o` there opens the reader (below); `r` opens the #lounge composer replying to it (`ChatState::begin_reply_to_article`, `ReplyTo::Article`), and the sent message carries `> @sharer: 📰 Title` with no `reply_to_message_id`, since there is no message to point at. `r` on the selected article in the News room, or in the reader, starts the same reply and switches to #lounge through the one `news/input.rs::reply_in_lounge`.
- **The list** (`news/ui.rs`) is two rows a story with a blank row between stories: the title behind its unread dot, then `@sharer · 4m · example.com · 7 min read`. The selected story opens out with its link, up to three bullets of its summary (two rows each, hung under the text after the bullet as the reader sets them, the second cut with an ellipsis) and, on a list 90 or wider, its ascii art in a 12-column strip at the right. `ui::lay_out` is pure (every row, the selected story's row range) and the draw paints the window that keeps the selected story's last row in view (`ui::scroll_offset`), so moving down walks the list up the screen. Keys: `j`/`k`/arrows, `Enter` the reader, `s` save (below), `c` copy, `r` reply, `w` watch, `d` delete, `i` share, `/` mine only.
- **Saved for later** (`s` in the News room or the reader): a story goes on the reader's own shelf, one `article_saves` row (migration 249, `late-core/src/models/article_save.rs`), at most `NEWS_SAVED_LIMIT` (10). The list is `news::state::display_order`: the shelf first under a `── saved · 3 of 10 ──` rule, newest save first, then the feed under `── latest ──` less what is on the shelf, so a saved story appears once and the feed's copy of it (the fresher body status) is the one shown; with nothing saved there are no rules. A saved story stays after it leaves the snapshot and the reader, the watch, a reply and a delete all resolve a story through `news::State::find_article` (snapshot, then shelf, then the story the open reader is on, so a story unsaved in the reader can be saved again and its `w`, `r` and `d` still reach it; those keys act before the reader closes). The shelf rides `ArticleEvent::SavesLoaded` as full cards (`ArticleService::shelf_task` with `ShelfStep::Load`, which attaches sharers the way the snapshot does), loaded at session start, on every News room visit (`list_articles`, the replica-ready refresh for a delete on another replica or a body that landed) and after every save or unsave; each load replaces the shelf. `s` moves the story between sections at once and fires the write (`ShelfStep::Save` / `Unsave`). A session's shelf steps, its loads included, run one at a time in the order asked (each `shelf_task` waits on the last one's handle, `news::State::queue_shelf_step`), so an unsave pressed while its save is still out lands after it and no load publishes over a later write. A write that fails is logged and re-reads the shelf, which takes back the move the session made ahead of it. After `s` the cursor stays on the row, so the story that followed is under it ("keep this, move on"). A full shelf refuses in the session with "Saved is full: 10 stories" before any write, and the cursor stays where it was; `ArticleSave::save` checks and inserts in one transaction under a `('news_save', user_id)` advisory lock, so two sessions saving together cannot shelve an eleventh, and a refusal there republishes the shelf, which takes the story back off, then raises `ArticleEvent::SaveRefused` for the banner. A delete cascades through every shelf (`Deleted` re-reads the deleter's own). The shelf's rows carry no unread dot: the dot means "new since your last visit", which is the feed's. `metrics::record_news_save` labels `late_ssh_news_saves_total` by the closed `NewsSaveOutcome` (`saved` / `unsaved` / `full`); `full` counts both refusals, the session's (`ArticleService::record_refused_save`) and the table's. The shelf is the reader's own and never rides `all_articles` (the live strip, the Zen headlines, the paper read the snapshot only).
- **The reader** (`news/reader/`, `state.rs` / `ui.rs` / `input.rs`, shaped like the paper modal) opens a story's text: `Enter` on a News card, and `o` or a click on the live strip or a Live panel row for any story the watch player does not take (`news/input.rs::open_article`, below, hands it to `ChatState::open_reader_for_article`, a thin delegation to `news::State::open_reader`, which marks that article read: `news::State::mark_article_read`). `news::State` owns it as an `OpenReader`, the `ReaderModal` together with its outstanding body request; `render.rs` reaches the modal through `ChatState::reader`, `app/input.rs` through `has_reader` / `close_reader` for its keys and Esc, and for a page change or another modal. A ready body shows the title, a meta line (`example.com · 7 min read · shared by @mat 2h ago`), a rule, and the article text in one column at most 80 wide, its links numbered `[n]` in first-appearance order (the first nine) with a Links list after; a pending or unavailable one shows the hero art, the same header, the summary bullets, and `Fetching the article…` or `Read on the web: <url>` (`Watch: <url>` on a YouTube story, whose body is never fetched). The layout is pure (`ReaderModal::lay_out`, lines carry `ReaderInk`, colours are picked in the draw) and kept between frames until the width, the body, or the meta line's relative time changes; the draw turns only the rows in view into lines. Scrolling, the scrollbar, its drag, and the mouse gestures are the shared `ScrollView` (`app/common/scroll_view.rs`, also the paper's); the reader owns its page size (the body's height less a line, for `Space`/`b` and a track click). Keys: `j`/`k`/arrows/wheel a line, `Space`/`b` a page, `g`/`G` the ends, `1`-`9` copy link `[n]`, `c` copy the story's link, `w` on a YouTube story hand it to the watch player (below; closes the reader), `r` reply and `d` delete (both close the reader), `s` save it for later or let it go (the reader stays up), `N` select it in the News room, `Esc` or `[x]` close; everything else is swallowed.
- **Watching.** A YouTube story (`svc::is_youtube_url`) plays in the session's watch player (`../../video/CONTEXT.md`: the player, the setup box, both gates, the pair messages). Every YouTube card reads `watch` in `AMBER` for everyone (`ui::reading_label`, the closed `ReadingLabel`; whatever its body row says). `w` on one (the News room, where it goes ahead of the global Bonsai `w` only on a video card, or the reader) is `news/input.rs::watch_article`: the story's `WatchRequest` (`news::State::watch_request`, `WatchSource::NewsArticle`, found through `find_article`) goes to `video::svc::watch`, which opens the player when both gates pass and the setup box otherwise; a story that opened the player is marked read. `o` on the live strip or a Live row (`news/input.rs::open_article`) opens the player straight away when the session can play (`video::svc::can_play`) and the reader otherwise, never the setup box.
- **Resuming.** A story closed partway through picks up where it was left, on any machine and any `late`: the position lives on the reader's `article_reads` row (`watch_position_s`, `watch_duration_s`, migration 247; `ArticleRead::save_watch_position` / `clear_watch_position`), rides every reads load (`ArticleReadRow.watch_position` on `ArticleEvent::ReadsLoaded`) into `news::State::watch_positions`, and is what the player opens from (`news::State::watch_position`). What a closing player leaves (`Player::memory`, decided in `../../video/`) comes back as `news::State::remember_watch`, and every checkpoint while it plays as `news::State::save_watch_position`: each applies to the session's map at once (the card shows the progress before the write lands) and fires the write as a task (`ArticleService::save_watch_position_task` / `clear_watch_position_task`, each republishing the reads so the reader's other sessions follow). A card the reader is partway through reads `▶ 23:14 / 48:30` in place of `watch` (`ReadingLabel::Resume`, amber like `watch`).
- **Bodies.** After a share's `Created` is out, a task of its own (out of reach of the abort handle the composer holds, so cancelling the composer never cuts a fetch short) fetches the page for the reader inside the share's 5-minute budget (`ArticleService::store_body`, which owns the step's log and `metrics::record_news_body_fetch`; the fetch and the DOM walk are pure work in `news/body.rs`). A YouTube or X share gets an `unavailable` row with no fetch. Everything else is a GET (`User-Agent: late.sh (https://late.sh)`, 5 redirects, 10 s, at most 2 MB, `text/html` or `application/xhtml+xml` only), Readability (`dom_smoothie`) over it, and a walk of the article into `ReaderLine`s (`h1`-`h6`, `p`, `blockquote`, `pre`, `li`, `hr`, links made absolute; images, figures, tables, scripts, and nav dropped), capped at 2,000 lines or 200 KB of text and closed with `… continues on the web`; each line of a code block counts on its own and an oversized block is cut inside, so no `pre` carries the reader past the cap. Only UTF-8 is read: a page in another encoding is `unavailable` (`encoding` on the metric) rather than stored garbled. Any failure (the closed `BodyFetchFailure`, labelled on the metric and logged, never stored) stores `unavailable`, and none of them reaches the sharer: the share already succeeded. **The fetch only connects to public addresses**, under the house rule in `app/common/public_net.rs` (the one Files' downloader holds too): its client resolves through `PublicOnlyResolver`, which drops every non-public answer, checks a literal IP host before the request and on every redirect (five redirects followed, the sixth refused), and uses no proxy; the page's text is shown to everyone, so this is what stops a share from reading the cluster's insides.
- Bodies live in `article_bodies` and never ride the snapshot: the feed's `LEFT JOIN` brings only `ArticleFeedItem.body` (`BodyStatus::{Pending, Ready { reading_minutes }, Unavailable}`, no row is pending), and the reader loads its one row by id on open (`ArticleService::load_body_task`). The answer (`BodyReply::Loaded` or `Failed`) comes back on a `oneshot` the asking session holds inside its `OpenReader`, never on the shared `ArticleEvent` broadcast, so no other session (the same user's on another device included) ever takes it, and closing or reopening the reader drops a late one. `BodyLoad::Open` counts `metrics::record_news_reader_opened`; the re-ask after a pending body lands (`BodyLoad::Landed`) is the same open and does not. A load that fails is logged in the service and turns that reader to its summary with a banner. A body landing fires `articles_changed` (statement trigger, migration 246), so cards flip from pending to `7 min read` or `link` on every replica (a YouTube card reads `watch` throughout), and an open reader still waiting re-asks, once, when its snapshot row stops being pending. The body sweep runs hourly on every replica (`start_body_sweep_task`, two idempotent statements): a story shared more than six minutes ago with no body row had its fetch cut short (a restart mid-fetch, a store that failed) and is settled as `unavailable` (`ArticleBody::settle_pending_before`, which on its first run also settles every story shared before bodies existed), and a ready body fetched more than 30 days ago is turned into an `unavailable` row with no text (`retire_fetched_before`), unless the story is on someone's shelf, since it was saved to be read; either way the card reads `link` and the reader shows the summary.
- A share has no chat message. Old #lounge rows whose body starts with `---NEWS---` are still in `chat_messages`; nothing writes, parses, or deletes them, so they render as plain text.
- Delete removes the article (its author, or an admin with an audit row); there is no chat-side cleanup.
- URL processing has a 5-minute timeout. Image ASCII fetch has byte, pixel, and time limits.
- News snapshot is global: the newest `NEWS_FEED_LIMIT` (40) articles, one `watch` per replica. It is refreshed only by `ArticleService::start_notify_worker` (subscribed in `main.rs`), fed `articles_changed` (migration 198, a statement trigger on any `articles` write, empty payload) by the process listener (`pg_listener.rs`); it re-reads on the resync after LISTEN is live and on every notify, a burst collapsing into one read. Share and delete never refresh it directly; the notify brings the change back to the writing replica like any other.
- The News badge is counted in the session, not in SQL: `news::state::unread_in_snapshot` counts snapshot articles the reader has no `article_reads` row for (own shares included; `news::state::Reads` is the session's set of read ids). Read is a row and nothing else: a visit to the News room (`mark_read`, `ArticleRead::mark_feed_read`) inserts a row for every snapshot article, opening the reader (`mark_article_read`) inserts that one article's and nothing else, and a brand-new user is seeded the way a visit reads (`ssh.rs`), so a first login never shows the back catalog as unread. `news_unread_label` renders a full snapshot as `40+`. The stored reads arrive as `ArticleEvent::ReadsLoaded` (one `ArticleReadRow` each) at session start and after either write, and after a video position is saved or cleared, bounded to the snapshot's articles and the reader's shelf (so a saved video still resumes once it has left the snapshot) and never a long reading history; rows only grow, so the session merges each load into what it holds and two writes' loads landing in either order never take a read back. The video positions riding the rows move, so each load replaces `news::State::watch_positions` whole. Until the first load `Reads::Loading` keeps the badge empty. The News list's dots read the reads held from when the room was entered (`news::State::marker`). The "N new articles in news" banner fires when a refreshed snapshot holds an unread article by someone else that the previous snapshot did not (`has_fresh_unread_from_others`); a session's first snapshot never announces. No per-user event is ever published for other users, so a write costs one list query per replica whatever the users table holds.
