# Realm Context

## Metadata
- Scope: `late-ssh/src/app/door/realm` — the house's one standing world: multiplayer (2-10) simultaneous-turn territory conquest, one season at a time, on real Earth or a drawn world; plus persistence in `late-core/src/models/realm_game.rs`, migrations 192-198 and 241-243, the map generator `tools/realm-mapgen`, and the committed assets `late-ssh/assets/realm/`.
- Domain: a long-lived (weeks) DB-durable game the server opens, numbered as a season, with the next one rising when it falls. Each player gets N action points per *realm day*; an action (claim a free territory / attack an owned one) **resolves the instant it is taken** and everyone sees it on their next snapshot. Races are first-come-first-served through the row CAS. A realm day rolls over at the world's `reset_hour_utc` (`HOUSE_RESET_HOUR_UTC`, 18) and only governs the budget and the idle clock. Win = conquering every rival (or, the same finish from the other end, holding every territory). Unclaimed land does not keep a realm open: mopping up ground nobody is contesting is bookkeeping, not conquest.
- Parent context: the root `CONTEXT.md` (the Games hub, `door/hub`, is the card it is entered from; the live strip, `app/live`, is where its moments show).
- Status: Active.

## 1. Shape

Three-layer split, daily-domain style (no live actor per game, load-validate-persist, everything survives restarts):

- **Rules as data.** `rulesets.rs::RULESETS` is the roster; every mechanic knob (min/max players, actions/day tiers by player count, kick days, probability constants, distance decay) is a `RealmRuleset` field. The **map is not**: it is picked per season (`WorldChoice`, Earth or drawn) and frozen into the snapshot beside the rules, because the same rules on another world is another game. `map::MAPS` holds two entries — `earth` (fixed, from Natural Earth data) and `generated` (drawn per game; see §2a). A game freezes a serde `RealmRulesetSnapshot` into its state at the opening, so editing a ruleset never mutates running games. New rulesets = new array entries, never new branches. Fields added since the first landing carry `#[serde(default)]` so games frozen before them still parse.
- **Instant engine.** `resolver.rs` owns the persisted state shape (`RealmGameState` in `realm_games.state` JSONB) and the pure `apply_action(state, map, actor, action, day, seed)`. One action = one point = one roll, applied on the spot. Seed = `blake3(game_id ‖ day ‖ revision ‖ actor ‖ target)`; RNG is a local SplitMix64 (portable forever). Because the revision is in the seed, a retry after a lost CAS is a genuinely new roll against the world as it actually is. Illegal actions (`ActionRejected`) are **refused**, not fizzled: nothing is spent, nothing is logged, the player is told and picks again. `sweep_idle` is the only other engine entry point: it dissolves a realm that every living player has been quiet in for `REALM_ABANDON_DAYS` (5, flat) and re-checks the end condition.
- **Attacking is the hard move.** Claiming free land is cheap; taking held land is not, and it gets harder the bigger the holder is: the strength ratio is raised to `attack_strength_exponent` (1.6), so a sprawling empire is a genuinely bad target and a cornered player is a soft one. On top of that an **opening truce** protects the early game — `attack_grace_days` (1) forbids every attack, `distant_attack_grace_days` (3) forbids attacks past your own border — both counted from `state.start_day`, both ruleset data. Claims are never blocked: the truce is there so players develop before anyone can be knocked out. `RealmGameState::attack_window(day)` is the single answer to "what is allowed right now", used by the engine, the rail, and the target table alike.
- **Routes are per landmass** (`Territory.landmasses` / `land_links`, built by `map::compute_landmasses` from the grid). A border only helps you if you are standing on the same landmass as the border: France borders both Brazil and Spain (French Guiana is France), and without this the atlas is a dry road from South America to Europe — which is exactly the bug that shipped, showing Europe as "on your border" from Brazil at full odds and letting a landlocked player walk off their continent. `ReachIndex` keys its Dijkstra on `(territory, landmass)`; holding a multi-part country means standing on each of its landmasses, which is right — you hold ground on both. Only landmasses a territory has a *border* on are kept (Earth is tens of thousands of islets; carrying them all would blow up the node set for nothing, and anything reached from a lone islet is reached by sea anyway).
- **No route is not a cheap route.** `RouteMode::None` / `Reach::UNREACHABLE` / `ActionRejected::NoRoute`: another landmass with nothing to sail from or nothing to land on is refused, not priced. The old fallback handed it `Reach::ADJACENT` — cost 1.0, "on your border", full odds — which is where that bug lived. The fallback now only covers a player holding nothing at all.
- **The newcomer shield scales with the world.** `RealmPlayer.joined_claimed` (percent of the map already taken, frozen at join) plus `newcomer_shield_max_days` (4 on standard, pace-scaled) stretch the `New` phase: the plain `attack_grace_days` for somebody who was here at the start, up to that plus the max at the join door (`join_max_claimed`, 50%), pro rata between. `state.shield_days(user)` is the one answer; the rampup window keeps its own length and moves along behind it. Serde-default 0 means games frozen before this behave exactly as they did. In the sims a latecomer now comes out of protection holding ~20-28 territories instead of the 1 they spawned on — what happens to them afterwards is endgame pressure, not arrival handicap.
- **Spawns prefer a land border, then free ground next to it** (`svc::spawn_player`, seeded from the game and the revision at the join, so two arrivals never land by the same draw). A lone island is a fine place to play from and a hopeless place to start from once sea crossings are real, and a spawn walled in by other people's borders has nothing to spend its shield on; both preferences fall back when nothing else is free.
- **Reach is what a route costs, not how many steps it has** (`ReachIndex`). Two ways to arrive, cheaper one wins:
  - *Overland*: Dijkstra from everything you hold, charging for each territory the path passes **through** — your own land is free, empty land costs `free_transit_cost` (1), and a rival's costs `enemy_transit_cost` (3). Marching past someone is the expensive way round, which is what makes a border worth holding. The target itself is never charged; you are taking it, not crossing it.
  - *By sea*: from a coastal holding, `km / sea_km_per_cost` (2000km per point). A strait is nearly free, an ocean is not. The target must have a coast — an inland country cannot be taken off a boat — and whether an inland *player* may sail at all is the `landlocked_sea_attack` option.
  - Odds pay `distance_decay^(cost−1)`, continuous, so a border move (cost 1) pays nothing and the sea's fractional costs work properly. The old `stranded` bonus is gone: an island player now just has sea costs like everyone else, which is what that special case was standing in for.
- **Fortifications** (`OPTION_FORTIFY`, on by default). `f` — and only `f` — spends a point on land you hold to raise it a level; the attack key refuses your own ground with a nudge rather than quietly digging, because a point spent on walls is a point not spent taking ground and nobody should spend one by reaching for the wrong key. It raises it a level, to `fort_max_level` (5); level one is simply holding the place and is never stored. Each level past the first multiplies an attacker's odds by `fort_defence_per_level` (0.88) and adds `fort_transit_per_level` (0.4) to the cost of marching past — so a fortified pass is worth going *around*, which the routing will do by itself. Taking a territory levels its walls, so offence is rewarded with a weak position and a frontier never sets. Walls fade with their holder's dormancy (the decay multiplier walks the bonus back towards nothing), because an abandoned fortress stalling a realm for everyone else is exactly the shape we spent the anti-quitting work avoiding.
  - **The economy is deliberately unfavourable**: four points of wall buy fewer than four points of attack (asserted in `resolver_test`), because a realm is won by taking ground. Digging in is for the pass you mean to keep or a day with nothing better. `simulation_test::digging_in_is_not_a_way_to_win` plays two pure turtles in a five-player game and requires that they never win and that the realm still ends — that test is the balance, not the arithmetic above it.
  - Shown everywhere, to everyone: the map draws each level as a step on that player's colour ladder (`state::PLAYER_PALETTES`, five shades, pale = open ground, dark = walled up), so solid-looking means solid and it is the only signal that survives a one-pixel country, the target table has a `walls` bar column and the rail card draws the same bars (`map_ui::fort_bar`, shared so the two cannot drift) plus what they cost an attacker, the standings total each player's walls so a turtle is visible, and the log records each dig.
- **Options** (`rulesets::OPTIONS`, frozen per game at their roster defaults, stored in the snapshot): `long_range_strikes` (on by default) and `landlocked_sea_attack` (off). They are data, so adding one is an array entry plus a place that reads it; a game frozen before an option existed takes the roster default rather than a rule nothing enforces. With long-range **off**, a move must stay inside `short_reach_cost` (2) — and that cap **lifts entirely for a player with nothing in range** (`anything_within`), so a short-reach game can never box someone into having no move.
- **`RealmState::tick()` returns `RealmTick { banner, changed }`**, and `changed` is the load-bearing half. A realm action resolves in a service task, so the event comes back on one frame and the reloaded board on a later one; reporting only the banner left the second frame waiting for the next keypress (claim a territory, be told you have it, watch the map keep showing it as somebody else's until you move). `changed` is set by: a new snapshot, any event, a board or log load landing, and the muster countdown crossing a second — the one thing on screen that changes with nothing arriving, so it asks for its own frames.
- **The house runs the season loop.** Nobody creates a realm: the sweeper, on every replica, opens the next world whenever none is open or active (`RealmService::open_season` → `open_world(house_spec)`). **The opening is claimed before anything is spent on it** (root CONTEXT once-only rule): `open_world` takes `RealmGame::try_lock_season_opening`, a transaction-scoped advisory lock, and under it rechecks that no world stands, reads the season (`next_season`), builds the spec for that season, pays the AI for a drawn world's names, and inserts, all in the one transaction. A replica that loses the claim, or finds a world standing, spends nothing and reads `RealmSeasonOpen::Standing` (counted in `late_ssh_realm_seasons_total`); an opening that dies half way rolls back and leaves no claim and no row. The insert lands the row **active** (`RealmGame::open_season`): there is no second write to promote it, so nothing can strand an `open` row that blocks every season after it. The table still holds the invariant on its own (`realm_games_one_live`, a partial unique index), whatever path a write takes. The row is the `system` user's (looked up by `SYSTEM_FINGERPRINT`; a tick before the lounge feed ensured it bails and the next tick opens the world) with an empty roster: the first player is a joiner like every one after. `house_spec` is the whole product choice in one place, as a `WorldSpec` (ruleset and pace as roster references, the reset hour, and the closed `WorldChoice` map): the standard rules at `HOUSE_PACE` (`fast`), `HOUSE_RESET_HOUR_UTC`, Earth for odd seasons and a drawn world (`GeneratedMapSpec::default`'s shape) for even ones. Tests open their own `WorldSpec` through the same `open_world`. The name column is never written and reads "Season N" (`game_name`).
- **Service.** `svc.rs::RealmService`, process-global (constructed in `main.rs` beside `DailyService`): watch snapshot + broadcast events + fire-and-forget mutator tasks + the notify worker + the 60s sweeper. `act()` is the hot path: load, roll, persist under the row CAS, archive the log line, emit `ActionResolved` (targeted, the actor's banner), republish the snapshot. The sweeper opens the next season when no world stands, gives a standing world from before chat rooms its room, rolls the world's day over at its `reset_hour_utc` (archiving the day it closes), dissolves a realm nobody has touched in five days, settles the pot of any finished game whose `payout` is still NULL, and deletes the chat rooms of worlds that ended over 30 days ago.
- **`news` is what the live strip reads** (`RealmGameState::news`, `RealmNews { at, kind }`): the write that made a moment stamps it into the row, so every replica's strip shows the same moment at the same instant (`door/realm/live.rs`, `LiveSource::Realm`). The moments: `Opened` (the world rose), `Joined` (who, in which colour), `Took` (who took which country, by name, from whom), `Fell` (who lost the last of their land, to whom), `Ended` (the conqueror, or nobody). A claim on empty land is not a moment, so it never knocks one off the strip; a fall outranks the take that caused it; the end outranks everything (`news_of_action`, `ended_news`). Usernames are carried in the news because a withdrawal removes the player from the roster.
- **Fan-out is derived from the database, on every replica** (root CONTEXT multi-replica rule). A trigger on `realm_games` (migration 241) fires `realm_game_changed` on every row write; `RealmService::start_notify_worker` re-reads the snapshot on each notify (`Refresh::RealmGames`, resync retried through `read_until_ok`). `publish` then diffs the snapshot it had against the one it just read and emits what changed: `GameChanged` for a moved revision, a changed status, payout or `chat_room_id` (so a board open on a world the sweeper just gave a room reloads and finds it), or a row that left the listing, `DayRolled` when `last_resolved_day` advanced on an active game, `GameFinished` when a game left the active list for the finished one, `SeasonOpened` for a new row still in its muster (every session raises it as a banner). The writer never sends those itself, so a write on replica A reloads boards and raises banners on replica B exactly as on A, and the same replica's own refresh after its own write derives nothing twice (publishes are serialised under `publish_lock`, and a snapshot that was never seeded derives nothing, so a resync after a reconnect does not announce every game as new). Only the two targeted events are sent by the writer: `ActionResolved` and `Error`, whose session is on the replica that ran the write. What must happen once (the #lounge headlines, the lounge lines, the payout) stays with the write that won the CAS. The 60s sweeper refresh is the backstop.

## 2. Persistence (late-core)

- `realm_games` (migration 192, with `chat_room_id` (`ON DELETE SET NULL`), + `reset_hour_utc` in 195, + `name` in 196, + `payout` in 242, + the `realm_game_changed` trigger in 241, + `season` and the one-live index in 243): `season` counts the worlds the house opened (0 on rows from before it did); `name` is the label player-made realms carried and is left at its blank default by the house — the read side (`game_name`) reads "Season N", so a rename would never have to rewrite rows. Otherwise: status open/active/finished/cancelled, `ruleset_id`, `reset_hour_utc`, `last_resolved_day` (the last realm day the sweeper closed out — DB-durable, never an in-process timer), state JSONB with top-level `revision` CAS (`stored_revision_eq_sql`, daily_match pattern). Now written by every action too, which is exactly what makes the first-come-first-served race honest.
- `realm_actions` (migration 193) is **gone** (dropped by migration 195). With instant resolution there is no queue to hide, and every action writes the game row anyway.
- `realm_days` (193, + `board` in 198): the per-day log archive. A day's row is rewritten as the day is played (`archive_day` upserts), so history survives even if the process dies mid-day; the game row keeps only the current day in `state.last_day`. Paged by the log view.
- `realm_game.rs` methods: `try_lock_season_opening` (the opening claim), `open_season` (the insert of an active world under that claim, refused while a world stands), `next_season`, `live` (the one open or active row), `list_open/active/finished_recent(24h)`, `list_active_for_sweep(now, limit)` (the per-row "is this game behind its own clock" test is in the SQL, from `reset_hour_utc`, so the per-tick bound only ever lands on games with work to do), `list_finished_unpaid`, `set_payout`, `attach_chat_room` (the world's room, `ChatRoom::get_or_create_realm_room`, recorded on the row; idempotent), `delete_stale_chat_rooms` (30 days after a world ended, voice channels with them; the `DailyMatch` shape), `update_state_cas`, `advance_day_cas`, `finish_cas`, `cancel`, `archive_day`, `load_day_logs`. `REALM_GAME_CHANGED_CHANNEL` is the notify channel the model owns.
- **The snapshot never builds a world.** `game_item` takes the territory total from `map::territory_total` (the spec's own count for a generated map, the static atlas for a fixed one), so re-listing every game after every action is a parse, not a generation; a generated world is built only when a board opens on it (`mapgen::map_for_spec`, cache of 24). A row whose state no longer parses is left out, logged and counted (`record_realm_snapshot_row_rejected`) the first time it goes missing on this replica, the daily `SnapshotRowError` shape.

## 2a. Generated worlds (`mapgen.rs`)

- **A generated map is a recipe, not a payload.** The game stores a `GeneratedMapSpec` — seed, continents, islands, territories, and the list of names — in `ruleset.map_spec`, and `mapgen::generate` rebuilds the identical world from it every time. A 400-territory world is ~1 MB of cells; what goes in JSONB is ~40 lines of JSON. Names are the one part that is *stored* rather than derived, because they may come from the AI service and that is not a pure function of the seed. The generator honours `territories` exactly (`mapgen_test`), which is what lets the snapshot count a world's land from the spec without building it.
- **The seed is the server's.** `svc::rand_seed()` draws it when the world is opened; the spec carries only the three counts. A seed anyone could choose would let them re-roll until their own spawn corner looked good.
- **The globe is Earth's globe.** Same equirectangular projection, same sphere, land held at ~29% of it whatever the parameters. So `sea_hop_km`, the distance decay and every other km-tuned ruleset constant mean the same thing on a generated world as on the real one — the parameters change the shape of a game, never how much world there is. Grid is 1024×512 (a quarter of Earth's linear resolution; invented coastlines do not need survey detail), mips built the same way.
- **How it is drawn.** Landmass centres by best-of-N farthest-point sampling (latitudes drawn in sine space, nothing past 72°); each mass grown by a cheapest-first flood over a shape field, so it is connected by construction however wild the field gets. The field is three layers, because any one alone reads as a blob: distance to the nearest of 2-4 **nuclei** strung along a random axis (a continent gets a long side and a waist instead of a middle), divided by a radius breathing with four **angular harmonics** (gulfs and headlands), all measured at a **domain-warped** coordinate — two low-frequency noise fields bend the ground before it is measured, which is what folds a smooth arc into bays, spits and the occasional isthmus — with a further fractal-noise term on the radius for detail down to cell scale. `continents_are_not_discs` pins it with the isoperimetric quotient (`4πA/P²`: 1.0 for a circle, ~0.6 for the first cut of this generator, 0.11-0.19 now, 0.014 for Earth's Afro-Eurasia on its four-times-finer grid); territories allocated per mass by *actual* area, then cut by multi-source Dijkstra from scattered seeds with per-seed speed drawn log-normal — which is what gives a world a few sprawls and many small countries. Area is summed from per-row cell areas (cos-latitude), population is area × a density drawn over three orders of magnitude.
- **Names.** `assets/realm/invented_names.txt` is a built-in pool of 1000 invented names; `svc::invent_names` asks the AI service first (batches of `NAME_BATCH` = 80, `NAMING_BUDGET` = 20s, one of `NAME_FLAVOURS` picked by seed so two worlds do not read as the same continent) and tops up from the pool for anything missing, rejected, or repeated. **The pool is not a degraded mode**: it is what tests and any keyless box always use, and a pool-named world is a normal world.
- **Handles, not statics.** Because a generated map is built at runtime, `map_by_id` and `map_handle` return `Arc<RealmMap>` and `RealmMap.id`/`display_name` are `String`. `map_handle(id, spec)` is the single lookup: fixed ids hit the `LazyLock` statics, `generated` hits `mapgen::map_for_spec`, which caches up to `MAP_CACHE_MAX` (24) built worlds keyed by spec hash and builds outside the lock.

## 3. Lifecycle & rules (standard ruleset)

- **A world musters for `REALM_MUSTER` (a day) before anyone can act.** Opening one announces it — a banner to every session (`SeasonOpened`, derived from the snapshot, so on every replica) and a `system` headline in #lounge (`ActivityKind::RealmSeasonOpened`, carrying `muster_label()` as `opens_in` so the deadline is Realm's own; `filter.rs::lounge_headline`: "A new realm rises: season N ... the first point is spent in a day. Press 3, then Realm, to take your spot") — and for that day the world is visible and joinable but frozen: map, spawns and roster all readable, every action refused with the countdown. A day, because the headline is for the whole house and every time zone should get to read it and take a spot before the first point is spent. The freeze is the **one rule the engine does not hold**: `resolver.rs` has no clock at all (it is day-quantised by design), so threading a wall-clock instant through `apply_action` and every simulation playout to enforce something that expires in minutes would be the wrong trade. It lives in `svc::act_at` and, for the pre-flight refusal, `state::act_on`/`fortify_on`. `svc::muster_left` is the single answer; there is no new column, since `realm_games.created` already says when the clock started. `act_at(now)` exists so tests need not wait the muster out (`sweep(now)` precedent); tests that are not about the muster wind `created` back two days (`svc_test::open_the_doors`).
- **There is no starting gun.** Opening a world makes it live, with nobody on it. Everyone arrives through the gates (`j` on the board's map while they are open, the colour picker, `join_game`), spawning on arrival into a war already in progress — no waiting room, no roster to assemble, no synchronised start. `join_game` accepts while the roster has room and the map is under `join_max_claimed` (50%); past that a newcomer would only be food, so the doors close (`RealmGameState::joinable`, surfaced as `RealmGameItem::joinable`/`claimed`). A realm short of a rival (`short_of_rivals`: fewer than `min_players` on the roster) keeps them open however much is taken, because it cannot be won until somebody arrives; closing them stranded a lone player in a war with no end but the dissolve.
- **Protection is per player, not per game** (`PlayerPhase`, counted from each player's own `joined_day`, which is the day their clocks start: the day they arrived, or for an arrival during the muster the day the gates open, `svc::first_playable_day`, so the frozen day never eats a shield or counts as quiet): **new** for `attack_grace_days` (1) — free land only, and untouchable; **ramping up** until `distant_attack_grace_days` (3) — neighbours only, and only neighbours may come for them; then **involved**, where anything goes both ways. `attack_allowed(actor, defender, hops, day)` is the single check, and it refuses in both directions, so arriving on day forty buys the same footing the founders had.
- **The daily budget follows the involved**, not the roster (`daily_base` → `involved_count`): a realm whose players are all still finding their feet pays the most generous tier, which is right when one or two people are alone on a whole world.
- **The day turning over is announced twice.** Every player on the roster gets `RealmEvent::DayRolled` — derived by each replica from `last_resolved_day` advancing in its snapshot, and carrying the game's *name*, because the session raises it as a banner wherever they are in the app ("The Realm calls you! (name)"), not only with the board open. And the lounge ticker gets one line (`svc::announce_day_rolled`, from the write that won the CAS), `ActivityKind::RealmCalls` → "realm <name> calls its players", attributed to no user because the summons is the game's. That line is gated on more than one living player: a realm somebody made and sat in alone would otherwise post about nothing, daily, for as long as the row lives. The repeat key is `realm-calls:<game>:<day>`, so a rollover announced twice posts once.
- **Reset hour** (`realm_games.reset_hour_utc`, 0-23, held by the column's CHECK): when this game's points come back, every day. The house world refills at `HOUSE_RESET_HOUR_UTC` (18), an evening in Europe and an afternoon in the Americas; the column stays per row because rows from before the house kept their own hour. Every surface writes it in both clocks (`svc::reset_hour_label`, `RealmState::viewer_tz`, mirrored from `App` like the chat pane's zone).
- Actions/day follow the involved count (above), by tier: 2-4 → 7, 5-7 → 5, 8-10 → 4 (`actions_per_day_tiers`, as written; the pace's `action_scale` multiplies them). A point is spent on the attempt, won or lost; a *refused* action costs nothing.
- Probability (pure fn `action_probability`): territory weight `w = (ln pop + ln area normalized)/2`; strength `S = 1 + Σ held weights` (the defender's after decay); claim `0.90 − 0.25w`; attack `(0.45·(2S_att/(S_att+S_def))^1.6 − 0.15w) × fortification`; then `× distance_decay^(cost−1)`; clamp to [0.05, 0.95] on your border, [0.01, 0.95] beyond it. The UI's projected % is the same function on the same state — it is the real number, it can just go stale the moment someone else acts.
- Exits: a voluntary leave frees the territories (the last player leaving cancels the world, and the next sweep opens the next one); nobody is kicked for going quiet, a dormant empire just decays (`decay_multiplier`) until someone takes it; losing your last territory = **Eliminated**, stays spectator. One alive player left (every rival conquered) finishes the game, as does someone owning every territory — the same finish reached from either end. Free land left on the map does not hold it open. A world nobody has marched into yet stands until somebody does: an empty roster is an invitation, not an abandoned game, so the first reset never dissolves it mid-muster. A roster with nobody alive dissolves it (no winner, no payouts), as does a realm every living player has been quiet in for `REALM_ABANDON_DAYS`.
- **Pace** (`rulesets::PACES`, `HOUSE_PACE` for the house world, frozen into the snapshot by `RealmRulesetSnapshot::at_pace`). Orthogonal to the ruleset: the rules say how a conquest works, the pace says how long it takes. `action_scale` multiplies the daily points, `day_scale` multiplies every window measured in days (opening protection, the week a win takes, dormancy, banking), and `pot_scale` multiplies the prize. Floors keep a blitz playable — a win you could reach in a day, or an opening with no protection, would be a different game rather than a faster one. Measured on five mixed policies: **epic 158 days, slow 68, normal 39, fast 17, blitz 12** (`simulation_test.rs`). `slow` is the rules exactly as written, and `normal` is the reference tempo whose pot is the plain per-player rate.
- **A finished world stays listed for `REALM_RESULTS_HOURS` (24)**, so everyone gets to read the result on the hub card and the strip while the next world musters, then drops out of the listing. The row is never deleted: the history and the archived day logs outlive the listing.
- **Ranked payouts** (migration 194, rescaled by 197). The pot is computed per game: `REALM_POT_PER_PLAYER` (3000) × `paying_players`, everyone who played as long as a win takes (`min_active_days_to_win`), floored at two. Counting the roster would let a colluder walk alts in without taking a turn, and counting the podium bar (`min_payout_active_days`) let nine alts clicking a few days each bank a whole 60/25/15 split; at the win bar every padded body costs as many days as the conquest. It is a mitigation, not a cure: the pot still scales with headcount, which is what alts fake. The cost accepted: an honest player knocked out before the win bar does not grow the pot. So a duel is worth 6k and a ten-player war 30k — placing in a crowded game is the harder feat and pays like it. Multiplied by the pace's `pot_scale` (epic 3.0 → blitz 0.25) and capped at `REALM_POT_MAX` (60k, because ten players at the epic tempo would otherwise compute a prize bigger than anything else on the platform pays). Split by tier (`payout_plan`): ≤3 winner takes all; 4-6 → 70/30; 7+ → 60/25/15, rounded to the nearest 50. That puts a realm win in the door-game milestone class (Green Dragon 10k, A Dark Room 15-20k) instead of below a daily Asterion escape (4000), where the old flat 2000 sat. The six `reward_templates` rows still own the claim policy (`per_event`, one payment per rank per game) and the game/payout_kind identity; `reward_chips` on them is the floor of each tier, and `credit_per_event_reward_template_amount` passes the computed figure. Ledger reason is `ChipMove::RealmConquest` (`realm_conquest`), which counts as earnings on the Top Chips board.
- **The pot is settled after the finish, and recorded** (`svc::settle_payouts`, `realm_games.payout`, migration 242). `finish_cas` makes the finish durable; the credits run after it, from the row as stored, and `payout` is written only once every place in the plan has been credited (`paid`), or the game turned out not to pay (`unearned`: a winner who did not clear the week or the territory floor; `dissolved`: no winner). A failed credit leaves `payout` NULL and the sweeper settles it on its next tick (`list_finished_unpaid`), the per-event claims making the retry pay nothing twice. Every place lands on the dashboard by outcome (`record_realm_payout`: paid, already paid, unearned, dissolved, failed). The results card reads the column: until it is set, the pot line says the chips are on their way rather than paid.
- **You can withdraw on your first day, and not after.** While you are `New` nothing has happened to you and nothing has happened because of you, so leaving is free; past that you are committed, because quitting used to hand the game to whoever was left. The last player in a realm may always pack the whole thing up (`RealmGame::cancel`), since there is nobody to hand it to. Stopping otherwise is something you do by not playing: your land stays yours and stays takeable.
- **Winning takes a week of play, against real rivals.** `can_win` wants `min_players` on the roster (a realm sat in alone is not a conquest) and `min_active_days_to_win` (7) *distinct days the player acted*, tracked as `RealmPlayer::active_days` — elapsed days would be farmable by waiting. Eliminating everyone sooner does not end the game (`check_end` returns `Ongoing`); you hold the field until the days are in. Both win conditions go through the same bar, which never bites on Earth (a conquest there is weeks of work) but stops a 12-territory map being swept in an afternoon with an obliging friend.
- **Missed days bank, on a ladder** (`standing`, `bank_rate`), one rung per missed day: the first banks 75%, each further one to `bank_missed_cutoff` (3) banks 50%, and each past that banks nothing, without taking back what the earlier rungs banked. The bank is capped at `bank_cap_days` (3) days' worth, which is what stops a fortnight away from becoming one flattening blitz. The day's allowance is **frozen at the first action of the day** (`RealmPlayer::actions_allowance`) — the gap it is computed from closes the moment you act, so without freezing it the bank would evaporate on the first spend.
- **An abandoned empire crumbles** (`decay_multiplier`): after `dormancy_grace_days` (3) quiet days a player's strength falls `decay_per_day` (10%) per further day, floored at `decay_floor` (0.40). Their land defends with less than its size suggests, so quiet players get carved up rather than freezing the game — and nobody is removed, which is what keeps "go quiet" from laundering into an exit. `effective_strength` is what the odds use; `strength` stays the raw figure.
- **The sweeper no longer kicks.** It rolls days over, and dissolves a game only when *every* living player has been quiet for `resolver::REALM_ABANDON_DAYS` (5 days, flat — not scaled by ruleset or pace, because what it measures is whether anybody still comes back). A player who never acted counts from their join day, so a realm nobody ever played clears itself too. That path pays nothing, so it cannot be farmed, and the next sweep opens the next world.
- **A game has to have been played to pay** (`payout_earned`: the winner cleared the week and holds at least `min_payout_territories`). Per player, `payout_eligible` also wants `min_payout_active_days` (3) of their own — the podium means "played and placed", not "was in the roster".
- Ranking (`final_ranking`): winner, then later exit day > exit territory count > seeded jitter; **leavers and kicked players are payout-ineligible** and the next eligible player moves up.
- Activity: two `system` headlines per world, attributed to nobody (`realm`): the rise (`RealmSeasonOpened`, with the season and the day's deadline) and the fall (`RealmSeasonEnded`, naming the conqueror as an `@mention` with the players and the pot, or that nobody held it; both say the next world rises within the day), each also a ticker line and keyed once per world in the repeat gate. Plus the conqueror's own #lounge chat line (`ActivityGame::Realm` → `GameWon`) and the ticker-only daily call (`RealmCalls`). Joins, takes and falls are not lounge lines: they are the strip's (`news`).

## 4. Map

> The atlas itself lives in `app::common::worldmap` (`data` = territories,
> grids, mips, Earth; `view` = viewport + painter). It was lifted out of here
> when `/map` wanted the same Earth. `door/realm/map.rs` is now just
> realm's roster (`MAPS`, `GENERATED_MAP_ID`, `map_handle`).


- `common::worldmap::data`: `WorldMap { territories, grid, mips }`. Two maps: `testmap` (12 hand-built territories; engine tests) and `earth` (242 Natural Earth countries; the standard ruleset). Territory `weight` is precomputed per map from that map's own pop/area maxima.
- Earth assets are COMMITTED and embedded: `earth_territories.json` (~30KB; id/iso/name/POP_EST/area/neighbors/center) and `earth_grid.bin` (~200KB RLE; header `RLM1 | u16 w=4096 | u16 h=2048 | u8 flags | u16 count`, body `(u16 run, u16 cell)`, `0xFFFF` water; `flags` bit0 reserves a future appended terrain plane — readers decode exactly w·h cells and ignore trailing bytes). ≈10km/cell at the equator. `mips` are majority-vote halvings (land beats water on ties) down to ≤160 cells wide, built at load for zoom-out.
- Generator: `tools/realm-mapgen` (standalone bin excluded from the workspace, so prod builds never see it; run with `--manifest-path tools/realm-mapgen/Cargo.toml`; dev downloads Natural Earth 50m admin-0 GeoJSON, not committed — see the bin's header for the exact commands). `--width` picks the grid resolution (default 4096 ≈ 10km/cell; `--width 8192` halves that at roughly 4x the asset size). The reader takes w/h from the asset header, so a finer map needs no code change. Scanline rasterization, raster-derived land adjacency (75 island countries are jump-only), zero-cell microstates force-painted one cell, `--check` validates round-trip + adjacency symmetry. Multi-part countries are ONE territory with unioned neighbors (France borders Brazil, because French Guiana does). That is the asset's model and it stays — but it is **not** a route: land adjacency is paired with the landmass it is on (`Territory.land_links`), so crossing France does not walk you from South America to Europe. See §3.

## 4a. History (the finished realm, walked day by day)

- **Every day keeps the board it closed on.** `realm_days.board` (migration
  198) holds a `DayBoard` — the roster, `(territory, slot)` pairs and the
  walls — written once at the rollover, which is the moment worth keeping: the
  map as it stood just before everyone's points came back. Written again at
  the finish, onto the day the game ended on, since there is no rollover left
  to carry it.
- **The snapshot carries its own roster.** A player who withdraws is *removed*
  from `RealmGameState::players`, so an index into the live roster means
  somebody else afterwards. `DayBoard::players` (`DayPlayer { user_id,
  username, color }`) makes a day self-describing: it replays in the colours
  it was played in, and it can still name somebody the game has forgotten.
  Territories point at slots in *that* list, because a uuid is 36 bytes and
  there are up to 242 a day; free land is absent rather than recorded.
- **`archive_day` takes `Option<&board>`** and the upsert does
  `board = COALESCE(EXCLUDED.board, realm_days.board)`. The day row is
  rewritten on every action, so a live mid-day write must never replace the
  closing snapshot with a half-played one.
- **Three log entries were missing and now are not**: `Spawned` (arrivals were
  never logged at all — a game's opening positions were nobody's doing),
  `Left` with the ground it freed, and `freed` on `Kicked` (which only ever
  counted). Without these the map could not be replayed from the log however
  complete the actions looked. `RealmGameState::log` is `pub(super)` for
  exactly these two service-side entries.
- **The view** is `history_ui.rs`: `RealmView::History` in the Tab cycle.
  Its whole interface is the slider — `[`/`]` or `←`/`→` walk the days,
  `h`/`l` too, `g`/`G` jump to the ends — and there is deliberately **no pan
  or zoom**: the map always shows the whole world at the same frame, because
  walking the days is a comparison and two frames of a different picture do
  not compare. The track is drawn between literal `[` and `]` so the strip
  says how to move it without spending a line on saying so. The strip sits
  **above** the map and leads with the word — "history · 12 Sep · day 3 of
  45" — because this view and the live board draw the same world in the same
  colours, and what tells them apart must not be the last line on screen.
  Days show as dates; the stored number is days since the epoch, which is
  right to compute with and no use to read. It **opens on day one**: the last
  day's board is all but the live map, which is the one thing this view must
  not be taken for, while day one is a handful of spawns on an empty world.
  For "day 1 of N" to be true the whole archive has to be in — the pages are
  fourteen days each and the *first* page is the newest fourteen, so an
  unfinished run's oldest day is somewhere in the middle. `pull_history_archive`
  keeps asking for the next page from the frame loop while this view is open
  (driving it off "a page landed" missed the usual case, where the first page
  arrives while the board is still on the map), and until the run is in the
  strip says "reading the run in…" rather than calling its oldest known day
  the first. The log view still pages on demand, as it always did. It draws through a `HistoryFrame`
  gathered before drawing, which is what lets it be tested without a session.
  Days archived before boards existed have logs and no positions; they are
  skipped rather than drawn as an empty world, which would read as everybody
  losing everything that day.
- **No backfill, by nature.** Games finished before this have no positions to
  recover.

## 6. Critical invariants

- An action is resolved exactly once, by the write that wins the row CAS. The retry after a lost CAS re-rolls against the reloaded state (the revision is in the seed) — it never replays the old roll.
- A *refused* action changes nothing: no point, no log line, no state write. Only rolls are history.
- The projected percentage on screen and the rolled percentage come from the same `action_probability` call on the same state. If they ever diverge, the game is lying to players.
- Days are DB integers derived from the game's own `reset_hour_utc` (`svc::realm_day`); never an in-process timer, and never a global midnight.
- The engine (`resolver.rs`, `map.rs`, `rulesets.rs`) stays pure — no db/tokio imports; `svc.rs` owns all IO.
- `state.revision` only grows; CAS losers reload.
- Nothing a session is told about another player's write comes from the process that wrote it: `GameChanged`, `DayRolled`, `GameFinished` and `SeasonOpened` are derived from the snapshot diff after a re-read, so they are the same on every replica. A new fan-out event is derived there too, never sent from a mutation.
- At most one world is open or active at any moment, and the table holds that, not the sweeper: `realm_games_one_live`. A season is opened by exactly one replica's tick, the one holding the opening claim; the others spend nothing and read `Standing`.
- The strip's stamp for a moment is the row's own `news.at`, written by the write that made it, never a replica's clock at read time.
- A finished game's pot is settled from the stored row and recorded in `realm_games.payout`; a NULL there means the sweeper still owes it.
- Ruleset snapshots are frozen at the opening; never resolve against the live `RULESETS` entry. New ruleset fields need `#[serde(default)]`.
- Asset format: readers must keep ignoring unknown trailing planes in `earth_grid.bin` so terrain can land later without a version break.
- `LogEntry::Fizzled` and `FizzleReason` are retired but must stay in the enum: archived days from the queue era still deserialize through them.
