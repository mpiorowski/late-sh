# Realm: maps and generated worlds

The worlds a season is played on. Earth is a committed asset shared with
`/map`; a drawn world is a recipe rebuilt on demand. Routing over either is in
`RULES.md`.

## The atlas

> The atlas itself lives in `app::common::worldmap` (`data` = territories,
> grids, mips, Earth; `view` = viewport + painter). `/map` shares
> it. `door/realm/map.rs` is just
> realm's roster (`MAPS`, `GENERATED_MAP_ID`, `map_handle`).


- `common::worldmap::data`: `WorldMap { territories, grid, mips }`. Two maps: `testmap` (12 hand-built territories; engine tests) and `earth` (242 Natural Earth countries; the standard ruleset). Territory `weight` is precomputed per map from that map's own pop/area maxima.
- Earth assets are COMMITTED and embedded: `earth_territories.json` (~30KB; id/iso/name/POP_EST/area/neighbors/center) and `earth_grid.bin` (~200KB RLE; header `RLM1 | u16 w=4096 | u16 h=2048 | u8 flags | u16 count`, body `(u16 run, u16 cell)`, `0xFFFF` water; `flags` bit0 reserves a future appended terrain plane — readers decode exactly w·h cells and ignore trailing bytes). ≈10km/cell at the equator. `mips` are majority-vote halvings (land beats water on ties) down to ≤160 cells wide, built at load for zoom-out.
- Generator: `tools/realm-mapgen` (standalone bin excluded from the workspace, so prod builds never see it; run with `--manifest-path tools/realm-mapgen/Cargo.toml`; dev downloads Natural Earth 50m admin-0 GeoJSON, not committed — see the bin's header for the exact commands). `--width` picks the grid resolution (default 4096 ≈ 10km/cell; `--width 8192` halves that at roughly 4x the asset size). The reader takes w/h from the asset header, so a finer map needs no code change. Scanline rasterization, raster-derived land adjacency (75 island countries are jump-only), zero-cell microstates force-painted one cell, `--check` validates round-trip + adjacency symmetry. Multi-part countries are ONE territory with unioned neighbors (France borders Brazil, because French Guiana does). That is the asset's model and it stays — but it is **not** a route: land adjacency is paired with the landmass it is on (`Territory.land_links`), so crossing France does not walk you from South America to Europe (routing is in `RULES.md`).
- **Sea lanes** (`Territory.sea_lanes`, `data::compute_sea_lanes`, built with every map): a minimum spanning tree over the groups of territories that can walk to each other, each edge the closest pair of coastal territories between two groups, centre to centre. A lane is a crossing the campaign always allows, whatever `sea_reach_km` says, so no landmass is ever cut off from the rest. Most lanes are short enough that sailing reaches them anyway; on Earth the long ones carry Antarctica and the far southern islands, and on a drawn world the gaps between continents. It is a property of the map, not of a rule.

## Generated worlds (`mapgen.rs`)

- **A generated map is a recipe, not a payload.** The game stores a `GeneratedMapSpec` — seed, continents, islands, territories, the `Generator`, and the list of names — in `ruleset.map_spec`, and `mapgen::generate` rebuilds the identical world from it every time. Because of that, the algorithm is part of the recipe: a change to how worlds are drawn is a new `Generator` variant beside the old ones, pinned by its own fingerprint (`mapgen_test`), never an edit to one a live world was opened under. A spec stored before the field existed parses as `Flood`. A 400-territory world is ~1 MB of cells; what goes in JSONB is ~40 lines of JSON. Names are the one part that is *stored* rather than derived, because they may come from the AI service and that is not a pure function of the seed. The generator honours `territories` exactly (`mapgen_test`), which is what lets the snapshot count a world's land from the spec without building it.
- **The seed is the server's.** `svc::rand_seed()` draws it when the world is opened; the spec carries only the three counts and the generator. A seed anyone could choose would let them re-roll until their own spawn corner looked good.
- **The globe is Earth's globe.** Same equirectangular projection, same sphere, land held at ~29% of it whatever the parameters. So `sea_hop_km`, the distance decay and every other km-tuned ruleset constant mean the same thing on a generated world as on the real one — the parameters change the shape of a game, never how much world there is. Grid is 1024×512 (a quarter of Earth's linear resolution; invented coastlines do not need survey detail), mips built the same way.
- **How it is drawn: `Generator::Relief`** (every world opened now). Each landmass is planned as before (areas shared unevenly; a shape of 2-4 **nuclei** strung along an axis, four **angular harmonics** on the radius, a **domain warp** from two low-frequency noise fields, fractal noise on the radius), but placed and cut differently. Placement (`relief_center`): the first continent anywhere within ±40°, each later one off the coast of one already placed (centre gap 0.85-1.25 of the two radii), each island off a continent's shore, best of 24 draws by how clear it keeps of every mass, so the continents hang together as one world instead of being spread as far apart as they go. Every mass is a rise in **one elevation field** (`1 − shape cost`, the max over masses), plus a detail noise, minus a fall past 50° latitude so coasts curve away before the 72° cap instead of being cut off along it; the **sea level** is set so the land is Earth's share, highest ground first. The landmasses are what that leaves joined: rises that meet merge into one coast, and where the field hovers near the sea level it leaves capes, straits and offshore islets (pieces under `RELIEF_MIN_CELLS` sink; a spec with no islands keeps only the pieces a continent rose from). Countries (`partition_relief`) are an **eight-way** multi-source Dijkstra over rough ground (a noise field slows the race, so borders bend instead of running as diamonds), with **seed spacing** set by a slow noise field (`scatter_seeds_spaced`: half to twice the even spacing), so one region fills with small countries and the next holds a few sprawling ones; speeds barely vary, since a slow seed boxed in by fast ones comes out as an enclave. Measured on the default spec over 40 seeds: 8 worlds left a piece more than 3000km from any other shore, against 37 of 40 under `Flood`; the sea lanes join those. `continents_are_not_discs` pins the shape with the isoperimetric quotient (`4πA/P²`: 1.0 for a circle, 0.014 for Earth's Afro-Eurasia on its four-times-finer grid; a drawn continent must sit between 0.02 and 0.30). Area is summed from per-row cell areas (cos-latitude); population is area × a density drawn over three orders of magnitude.
- **`Generator::Flood`** (season 2's, kept until that world ends): each mass grown by a cheapest-first flood over the same shape field from a centre picked by best-of-N farthest-point sampling, which spreads the masses as far apart as they go and strands most of them past sailing reach; per-cell jitter on the flood gives the coasts a ragged fringe, and growth stops flat at 72°. Countries are a four-way Dijkstra with log-normal speeds, which draws diamonds and leaves enclaves.
- **Names.** `assets/realm/invented_names.txt` is a built-in pool of 1000 invented names; `svc::invent_names` asks the AI service first (batches of `NAME_BATCH` = 80, `NAMING_BUDGET` = 20s, one of `NAME_FLAVOURS` picked by seed so two worlds do not read as the same continent) and tops up from the pool for anything missing, rejected, or repeated. **The pool is not a degraded mode**: it is what tests and any keyless box always use, and a pool-named world is a normal world.
- **Handles, not statics.** Because a generated map is built at runtime, `map_by_id` and `map_handle` return `Arc<RealmMap>` and `RealmMap.id`/`display_name` are `String`. `map_handle(id, spec)` is the single lookup: fixed ids hit the `LazyLock` statics, `generated` hits `mapgen::map_for_spec`, which caches up to `MAP_CACHE_MAX` (24) built worlds keyed by spec hash and builds outside the lock.
