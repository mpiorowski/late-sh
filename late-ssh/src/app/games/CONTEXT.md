# Games Context

## Metadata
- Scope: `late-ssh/src/app/games`
- Last updated: 2026-09-10 (snooker joined `pool_core` as a third ruleset: `rules_snooker.rs`, the `SNOOKER_12FT` table, and its own ball id range. Also the shooter's-eye `table_3d.rs`.) Previously: 2026-09-09 (added `pool_core`: the billiard kernel behind daily eight-ball and nine-ball)
- Purpose: shared game-domain primitives and services used across game surfaces (Arcade, the house tables in `app/lobby/house`, and the Daily correspondence domain in `app/lobby/daily`).

## Source Map
- `mod.rs` declares shared game modules only.
- `cards.rs` defines card ranks, suits, `PlayingCard`, and ASCII card rendering themes (`Minimal`/`Boxed` one-line faces, `Outline` five-line faces, plus backs, empty slots, and a stock counter) used by Solitaire, the house card tables, and daily briscola. It renders cards and nothing else: there is no shared deck or shuffle here, and each game owns its own rank set and point values (briscola's ten-rank order generalizes to no other game).
- `chips/svc.rs` owns the Late Chips economy adapter: login ensure, bet debits, payout credits, floor restore, Activity-driven daily puzzle rewards, and reward-template claims for room-game daily/cooldown/per-event payouts. SQL stays in `late-core` models. Every mutation names a `ChipMove` variant (closed roster in `late-core/src/models/chips.rs`: ledger reason, debit floor, source kind, earnings flag) and flows through `UserChips::apply` or a dedicated path (`restore_floor`, `transfer_gift`, game payout claims); the `chip_user_changed` notify comes from `user_chips` triggers (migration 128), never from call sites.
- `chess_core/` is the surface-agnostic chess kernel (extracted from the demolished rooms chess table; see `devdocs/FRD-DAILY.md`). Daily chess and daily chess960 are its only consumers today, and they differ only in the opening position:
  - `types.rs`: `ChessColor`, `ChessPieceKind`, `ChessPiece`, `ChessGameResult`, `ChessMoveSpec`, `ChessMoveRecord`, `ChessPieceRenderMode`, `piece_glyph`.
  - `rules.rs`: pure helpers over `cozy_chess::Board` (legal move generation, queen-promotion move resolution, SAN labels, piece-array projection, repetition counting), plus `random_chess960_board` (the shuffled back rank, bishops on opposite colours and king between its rooks, mirrored for Black) and `fen`, the one way a position is written down. **Castling has two gestures, one encoding.** cozy-chess is Chess960-native, so it generates a castle as the king capturing its own rook (`e1h1`): select the king, then the rook. `legal_moves` additionally emits the lichess/chess.com pair (the king pushed two squares toward the rook, `e1g1`) for every generated castle, so both squares light up as legal targets, and `legal_move_for` maps that pair back onto the rook square via the board's stored castle rights. **That second pair is offered from e1/e8 only** (`castle_king_landing` and `castle_rook_target` share the guard): a Chess960 king starting anywhere else has ordinary one-step moves onto c1/g1, so there it castles by taking its rook and nothing else. Everything downstream (SAN label, `board.play`, the persisted `move_history` from/to) sees only the king-captures-rook move, so history and last-move highlights stay uniform however the player castled.
  - **FEN is Shredder notation for both variants** (`rules::fen`, i.e. `{board:#}`): castling rights name the rooks' own files, `HAha` where standard FEN writes `KQkq`. `KQkq` cannot name a rook that does not stand on a1/h1, so a Chess960 position written that way does not read back at all; one notation for both means no caller has to know which variant it is holding. `str::parse` accepts either spelling, so FENs stored before the switch keep loading.
  - `board_ui.rs`: the tiered board renderer (`Tier`/`pick_tier`, `BoardCtx`, `draw_board`, mouse `square_at`, `king_square`). Callers pass a plain `[Option<ChessPiece>; 64]` plus display context, never a table snapshot; piece-graphics image ids derive from a caller-supplied `placement_seed` Uuid (daily passes `match_id`; other surfaces pass their own stable id).
  - `piece_art.rs`: embedded PNG piece graphics for Kitty/iTerm2/Sixel plus tier thresholds.
  - `cursor.rs`: orientation-aware board cursor movement and legal-target filtering.
- `pool_core/` is the surface-agnostic billiard kernel behind daily eight-ball and nine-ball (`app/lobby/daily/pool.rs`). Like `chess_core` it knows nothing about matches, rows or screens, so a live pool hall as a house table would reuse it whole. **No new dependencies**: there is no mature billiards crate in Rust, and `rapier2d` is not one — a 2D rigid body carries a scalar rotation, and draw/follow is spin about a *horizontal* axis, so it cannot express them at all.
  - `table.rs`: `TableSpec`, every dimension and coefficient in SI — which is why snooker turned out to be a constant plus a ruleset rather than a rewrite. Presets `BAR_BOX_7FT` (pool's default, an amateur bar box), `PRO_9FT`, and `SNOOKER_12FT`, resolved by name through `preset()`. The spec also carries the baulk line and the D, because a snooker baulk line is not a quarter of the table and the rule and the painted marking have to be one number. **One** geometry builder emits cushions and pocket mouths together so they cannot drift apart; a pocket is the chord between its jaw tips and potting is the centre crossing it, so there is no capture radius to tune and a ball can never be both on the table and in a pocket.
  - `ball.rs`: ball state and the four motion states (stationary / spinning / sliding / rolling), each evolved in closed form. 2D velocity paired with **3D** angular velocity — the Han (2005) model `pooltool` implements, and the pairing is the whole point. Rest epsilons here are *physical* (0.1 mm/s), not floating-point: friction is asymptotic, so round-off-scale thresholds mean balls creep forever and a settling cluster generates contacts without the clock advancing.
  - `collide.rs`: ball-ball (with cut-induced throw) and ball-cushion impulses. Both cushion impulses stay in the tilted contact frame; resolving the normal along the horizontal while building friction against the contact normal *creates energy*, which showed up as a strike settling into bouncing between two rails forever.
  - `sim.rs`: the shot loop and the determinism guarantee. Motion inside a step is the exact closed form; only the contact *time* is numeric, by bisecting a boolean predicate for a **fixed 40 iterations** — no convergence test, so the work done is a pure function of the state. The textbook ball-ball quartic is near-double-rooted on grazing approaches, which is exactly the tunnelling and `t≈0` stall case. No `HashMap`/`HashSet` anywhere (iteration order must be positional), no `rand` (that lives in `rack.rs` behind a seed), every loop bound a constant.
  - `cue.rs`: the strike model (level cue, so no masse and no jumps), the miscue limit, `PowerBand` (light / normal / strong, each a ceiling the pointer's travel is spread across — a terminal has a few dozen rows to spend, and stretching 0-12 m/s over them puts a safety and a break a few pixels apart), and `ShotMode` — what the pointer is currently steering, with the hint strings the board shows verbatim. **Modes, not stages**: nothing about a shot is sequenced, and the reason it is a mode rather than a held key is that a terminal reports no key-up without the Kitty keyboard protocol. Both live here rather than in the board screen because they are part of the shot *model*, not one surface's input handling.
  - `shot.rs`: `Shot` (the persisted inputs), `RackState`, `ShotOutcome` (what the rules layer sees — deliberately free of physics types), and `Timeline`/`sample`. Timelines are never persisted: a shot is thousands of steps, and re-deriving beats storing.
  - `rack.rs`: seeded racks, and the only place randomness enters — its own SplitMix64, because `rand` offers no cross-version stability guarantee and a stored seed has to mean the same thing next release.
  - `rules_snooker.rs`: the full frame — fifteen reds, colours re-spotted while a red is up then taken in order, real foul values, play-again, and free ball. **The one ruleset that scores**, which is why `Ruling` has carried `points` and `balls_to_spot` since before there was anything to use them. Two decisions worth knowing: **nomination is inferred** (the ball you hit first is the one you nominated, which is how the game is played among friends, is unambiguous after the fact, and makes free ball need no new input), and **snookered is computed by the caller** (`is_snookered` tests the two grazing paths to each ball on rather than the line between centres — the rule asks whether *both extreme edges* are covered, and a ball you can only clip is still a ball you can hit). Snooker's balls take their own id range, 16..=30 reds and 31..=36 colours ascending by value, so nothing is ambiguous across rulesets: a pool ball's id is its printed number, and a snooker ball has no number.
  - `rules.rs` + `rules_eight.rs` / `rules_nine.rs`: `PoolRules`, a closed enum with exhaustive matches like the daily roster. The rules layer sees only `GameState` and `ShotOutcome`, never a physics type, so every ruleset is testable from struct literals with no table and no simulation. `Ruling` carries `points` and `balls_to_spot` from day one so snooker is a third file plus a variant, not a trait change.
  - `canvas.rs`: a half-block drawing surface (`▀` with fg/bg, so pixels come out near-square). Same trick as `app/lobby/house/image_render.rs`, but a surface you draw *into* rather than an image converter, and it lives here because `games` may not depend on `lobby`.
  - `table_3d.rs`: the shooter's-eye view of the whole table, `v` on the board. **A raycaster, not a 3D renderer**: everything worth seeing lies on one plane, so instead of projecting geometry it walks the *screen* and asks each pixel what it is looking at — cast a ray, hit the cloth, look up the spot. That removes near-plane clipping, the polygon rasteriser and the horizon special-case in one go (rays above the horizon simply never hit the plane), and it hands back the reverse mapping the mouse needs for free: `Eye::to_table` is an exact twin of `View::to_table`, not an approximation. Only the balls are projected, since they are the only things standing off the cloth; they are painted far to near so a ball in front covers one behind it, which is the point of the view. Each is a **sprite standing on its contact point** rather than a disc projected from its centre: the vertical is scaled harder than the horizontal, so a ball's own three centimetres of height came out as several pixels of lift and balls near the far rail floated clean over it into the room. Balls come from `table_ui::paint_ball` so they look like the same balls in both.
  - `table_ui.rs` / `cue_ui.rs`: the table overview and the shooter's-eye cue panel. The panel's balls and its sighting line take a *share* of the panel rather than a fixed size, and the board gives the panel a share of the board (`pool_ui::panel_width`), so the one part of the screen that exists to be looked at closely grows with the terminal. Balls are drawn **larger than life** with true positions — a true-scale ball is under two pixels. The exaggeration is a fixed number of *pixels* added rather than a factor multiplying, because a factor keeps inflating forever: a terminal big enough to draw the table properly ended up with balls half again as large as the table said. Adding a pixel and a half doubles a two-pixel ball and is a rounding error on a twelve-pixel one, and it means the renderer never has to know which game it is drawing. A solid is a plain disc and a stripe is painted the way a real one looks from above: the hue a band across the middle, both ends white, which degrades by itself from two clean stripes down to a mostly-white ball. Separation between neighbours (a fresh rack has every disc overlapping) is the ball's own outermost shell, shaded in its own hue — an edge, not an outline. It must live *inside* the disc: a ring of cloth painted just outside each ball separated them beautifully in isolation and had every ball carve a bite out of the neighbour drawn before it, so a cluster came out as rectangles with the stripes hanging off the side. The physics always uses the true radius; nothing in either file feeds back into the simulation. The table keeps its aspect ratio, so whatever the fit leaves over is painted `SURROUND` by `draw_bed` itself rather than left to the caller's background colour — as cloth it read as a green table of the wrong shape. `cue_ui::draw` returns a `PanelHit` (target ball, cue ball, their radii) because the panel's layout is also the board screen's click map: the part of the shot you click is the mode you arm. The cue ball's face there is drawn **magnified** — the whole ball is the half-radius `MISCUE_LIMIT` allows — since a true-to-life face spends most of a small ball drawing the part nobody may strike. The cloth's chalk — baulk line, the D, the two spots — is a *predicate* (`marking_at`) sampled by both renderers rather than drawn twice: the overview walks its cloth pixels and the eye view already walks rays, so neither needs to know how to draw a line and the two cannot disagree about where the baulk line is. That line is the only one that means anything: it is `rules::head_string`, the same number the kitchen rule is enforced against. `ring` is the outer shell of the disc of the same radius, sharing `disc`'s pixel-centre test rather than walking a circle by angle: the parametric version was half a pixel off and the two disagreed about which pixels were the edge, which showed as cloth-coloured dots inside a ball's rim. Rails scale with the table (`RAIL_BALLS`, so `fit_area` runs its fit twice — once to learn a ball's size, once with the rail that follows from it), and **every pocket is drawn on the table's edge, straddling it**: centred where its own mouth chord meets the rail, radius reaching its own jaw tips. A corner's chord is the *diagonal* across the corner, so a corner is drawn on the corner point — `mouth/2` further out along the chord's normal — or it floats a ball's width inside the cloth. Ball numbers are painted from a 3x5 pixel font at the largest whole scale that fits inside the disc, because a borrowed terminal glyph is drawn at the cell's size however big the ball is. The font literals are written **high bit leftmost** so each one reads on the page the way it lands on the ball — the first cut had it the other way round and the `1` came out mirrored. Whether a rack paints at all is one decision for the whole rack (`PAINT_BALL_PX`), not per ball: every ball is drawn the same size, so deciding per ball against its own digits put painted numbers on the single-digit balls and terminal glyphs on the two-digit ones. Two digits spilling slightly past the edge is the smaller ugliness.

## Boundaries
- `games` must not depend on `arcade` or `lobby`.
- `arcade` owns solo Arcade screen/runtime/UI.
- `chess_core` and `pool_core` here own only rules, shared types, and the bare renderers; the daily domain owns match lifecycle/persistence and the board screen chrome.
- Shared primitives belong here only when more than one game surface needs them.
- Do not move house-table registries, table settings, or runtime state into `app/games`. Those are Lobby-owned; `app/games` is only for cross-domain primitives/services such as cards, chips, and the chess kernel.
